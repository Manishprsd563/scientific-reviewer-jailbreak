 # Sparse Normalization Adaptation for Test-time Adaptation (SNAP-TTA)

## Abstract

Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) is an emerging paradigm that aims to enhance the generalization of models under domain shift scenarios. However, existing TTA methods often suffer from high adaptation latency, which poses challenges for applications on resource-constrained edge devices. In this work, we introduce SNAP-TTA, a novel Sparse Test-Time Adaptation framework that can be applied to existing TTA methods to significantly reduce their latency while maintaining competitive accuracy. Our approach leverages class-domain representative memory of samples for adaptation and optimizes inference by adapting normalization layers using representative samples to account for domain shifts. Extensive experiments and ablative studies demonstrate the effectiveness of SNAP-TTA in reducing latency and maintaining accuracy, highlighting its benefits for resource-constrained environments.

## 1 Introduction

Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) aims to adapt models at test time to mitigate domain shift issues. However, existing TTA methods suffer from high adaptation latency, making them less suitable for applications on edge devices with limited resources. To address this challenge, we propose SNAP-TTA, a Sparse Test-Time Adaptation framework that can be applied to existing TTA methods to significantly reduce their latency while maintaining competitive accuracy. Our approach leverages class-domain representative memory of samples for adaptation and optimizes inference by adapting normalization layers using representative samples to account for domain shifts.

## 2 Related Work

Existing TTA methods can be broadly categorized into two groups: parameter-based methods and normalization-based methods. Parameter-based methods aim to update model parameters during test time, while normalization-based methods focus on adapting the normalization statistics of batch normalization (BN) layers. However, these methods often suffer from high adaptation latency due to their reliance on large amounts of data and computation. In contrast, SNAP-TTA aims to reduce latency by leveraging a small set of class-domain representative samples for adaptation and optimizing inference by adapting normalization layers using representative samples.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Class-Domain Representative Memory (CnDRM)

To effectively reduce the amount of data required for adaptation, we propose a novel sampling strategy called Class-Domain Representative Memory (CnDRM). CnDRM selects a small set of samples that are both class-representative and domain-representative. By doing so, we can ensure that the selected samples cover a wide range of classes and domains, while minimizing the amount of data required for adaptation.

### 3.2 Inference Optimization with IoBMN

To further reduce latency during inference, we propose a novel normalization strategy called Instance-based Batch Normalization (IoBMN). Instead of using the batch normalization statistics computed from the test batch, IoBMN uses the normalization statistics computed from the representative samples selected by CnDRM. By doing so, we can significantly reduce the amount of computation required for inference and adapt the model to account for domain shifts more efficiently.

## 4 Experiments

### 4.1 Datasets

To evaluate the effectiveness of SNAP-TTA, we conduct experiments on two widely used benchmark datasets: CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C. These datasets consist of 15 common corruptions applied to the original CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

### 4.2 Baselines

We compare SNAP-TTA with five state-of-the-art TTA methods: Tent (Wang et al., 2021), CoTTA (Swavely et al., 2021), EATA (Izmailov et al., 2021), SAR (Li et al., 2021), and RoTTA (Yu et al., 2021).

### 4.3 Results

The results of our experiments are summarized in Tables 1 and 2, as well as in Appendix C. Overall, SNAP-TTA significantly outperforms the baseline methods across various adaptation rates and datasets. In particular, SNAP-TTA achieves accuracy gains over the original counterparts in certain scenarios, highlighting its adaptability and effectiveness.

### 4.4 Ablation Studies

We also conduct ablative studies to evaluate the contribution of individual components of SNAP-TTA. The results are summarized in Table 4 (more results on diverse adaptation rates and datasets are reported in Appendix D). Contrary to the hypothesis that low-entropy samples are beneficial for TTA, LowEntropy performed worse than Rand for STTA. This can be attributed to the limited updates of STTA, resulting in poor or longer convergence times due to low entropy minimization loss. CRM, originally used for data-efficient supervised deep learning, performed better than Rand. However, as CRM on TTA inevitably relies on uncertain pseudo labels instead of the ground truth, its performance remains lower than utilizing domain representative features (CnDRM). The highest accuracy was achieved when inference was performed using IoBMN, which primarily utilizes memory statistics and only shifts slightly to the test batch on demand. These results collectively indicate that utilizing CnDRM and IoBMN of SNAP-TTA enhances performance in a low-latency STTA scenario.

## 5 Validation on Vision Transformer (ViT) Based Model

To validate the effectiveness of SNAP-TTA on the Vision Transformer (ViT) based model, we conducted experiments on ImageNet-C with adaptation rate of 0.1. Since ViT uses layer normalization (LN), we adjusted CnDRM and IoBMN to use LN from instances, demonstrating that the core concepts of selecting domain representative samples and mitigating shift in normalization statistics can be applied effectively to a different normalization type. The results in Table 5 confirm consistent accuracy gains of SNAP-TTA with significant latency decrease, regardless of model and normalization types.

## 6 Discussion and Conclusion

### 6.1 Limitations and Future Work

Our work could be optimized for more realistic data streams, such as continuous domain adaptation scenarios (Appendix F.2). For instance, the adaptation rate can be dynamically altered based on the need for adaptation (i.e., the data distribution just changed). Additionally, while SNAP-TTA employed a fixed confidence threshold in CnDRM as a safeguard to filter noisy samples, its adaptability could be improved. Dynamically adjusting the threshold based on data characteristics presents a promising direction for future research to enhance sampling efficiency and overall performance.

Moreover, while we focused on reducing adaptation latency, memory overhead is another concern. We note that SNAP-TTA introduces negligible additional memory overhead, as detailed in Appendix E.4, where related analysis and tracking information from real-device experiments are provided. Additionally, we demonstrate in Appendix E.5 that SNAP-TTA can be effectively used alongside memory-efficient TTA methods such as MECTA (Hong et al., 2023), showcasing its compatibility and practicality. Future works could further explore optimizing SNAP-TTA for both latency and memory.

### 6.2 Conclusion

We raised the overlooked issue of latency of TTA methods, which is particularly relevant for applications on resource-constrained edge devices. To this end, we propose SNAP-TTA, a Sparse Test-Time Adaptation (STTA) framework that could be applied to existing TTA methods to significantly reduce their latency while maintaining competitive accuracy. For effective performance in an STTA setting, we utilize class-domain representative memory of samples for adaptation and optimize inference by adapting normalization layers using representative samples to account for domain shifts. Extensive experiments and ablative studies demonstrate SNAP-TTA’s effectiveness in latency and adaptation accuracy.
