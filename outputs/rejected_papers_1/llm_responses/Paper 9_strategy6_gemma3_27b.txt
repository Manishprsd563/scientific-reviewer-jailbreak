## Review of "SNAP-TTA: Efficient Sparse Test-Time Adaptation via Domain-Representative Sampling and Normalization Alignment"

This is a strong paper addressing an important and often overlooked aspect of Test-Time Adaptation (TTA): computational efficiency. The authors convincingly argue that many TTA methods suffer from high latency, making them impractical for resource-constrained edge devices. SNAP-TTA offers a compelling solution by intelligently reducing adaptation frequency through domain-representative sampling and aligning normalization statistics.  The paper is well-written, thoroughly evaluated, and presents a significant contribution to the field.

**Strengths:**

* **Novelty & Significance:** The focus on latency in TTA is genuinely novel. While much research focuses on accuracy, this paper highlights the practical limitations of computationally expensive methods. Addressing this gap is highly significant for real-world deployment. The approach of leveraging domain-representative samples and normalization alignment is creative and effective.
* **Technical Soundness:** The technical foundations of SNAP-TTA are solid. The authors clearly explain the rationale behind their choices and provide a rigorous methodology.  The use of CnDRM and IoBMN is well-justified, and the concepts are clearly explained.
* **Empirical Validation:** The experimental setup is comprehensive and rigorous. The authors evaluate SNAP-TTA on multiple datasets, compare it to strong baselines, and perform detailed ablation studies. The consistent performance gains across different configurations and datasets provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The inclusion of ViT experiments demonstrates the generalizability of the method.
* **Reproducibility:**  The authors demonstrate a commendable commitment to reproducibility by providing code, data, hyperparameters, and setup instructions. This is crucial for allowing other researchers to verify and build upon their work.
* **Related Work & Positioning:** The paper provides a comprehensive and well-structured discussion of prior work, clearly situating SNAP-TTA within the existing landscape of TTA methods.
* **Ethics & Broader Impacts:**  The authors thoughtfully consider the ethical implications of their work, addressing data provenance, licensing, and safety concerns responsibly.

**Minor Suggestions/Potential Areas for Future Work (already acknowledged by the authors):**

* **Dynamic Adaptation Rate:** The authors acknowledge the potential for dynamically adjusting the adaptation rate based on data distribution changes. Exploring this further would be valuable.
* **Real-World Data Streams:**  While the dataset used is a standard benchmark, extending the evaluation to more realistic, continuous data streams would further strengthen the paper.
* **Memory Overhead Optimization:** While the paper notes negligible overhead, continued optimization for memory efficiency remains a worthwhile pursuit.



**Overall Assessment:**

This is an excellent paper that makes a significant contribution to the field of Test-Time Adaptation. The authors address an important practical challenge, propose a novel and effective solution, and provide a thorough and rigorous evaluation.  The paper is well-written, clearly explained, and highly reproducible. **I strongly recommend accepting this paper.**
