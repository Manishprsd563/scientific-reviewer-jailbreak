The research paper under review introduces a novel hybrid batch training strategy aimed at enhancing the performance of information retrieval (IR) systems across monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual settings. The authors address the critical challenge of developing robust IR models capable of handling diverse languages and retrieval scenarios by proposing a method that combines monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs during fine-tuning.

### Key Contributions:
1. **Novelty / Originality**: The paper presents an innovative approach to training IR systems, moving beyond incremental improvements by introducing a hybrid batch strategy that integrates both monolingual and cross-lingual data.
2. **Significance / Impact / Usefulness**: The proposed method demonstrates significant potential in improving multilingual retrieval performance, contributing valuable insights for both academic understanding and practical applications.
3. **Technical Soundness / Correctness**: The methodology is technically robust, with a clear theoretical foundation and rigorous experimental design involving established datasets like XQuAD-R, MLQA-R, and MIRACL.
4. **Empirical / Experimental Validation**: Comprehensive experiments validate the approach, showing consistent improvements over monolingual and cross-lingual baselines across various metrics (MAP, Recall@10).
5. **Reproducibility / Transparency**: The paper provides sufficient details and resources to ensure reproducibility, though additional transparency on dataset coverage and limitations would enhance clarity.
6. **Related Work & Positioning**: The discussion of prior work is thorough, clearly situating the contributions within existing literature.
7. **Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety**: While addressing societal biases in training data is noted as an important future direction, current experiments focus on performance metrics without delving into ethical implications.

### Strengths:
- **Innovative Training Strategy**: The hybrid batch approach effectively balances monolingual and cross-lingual learning, leading to improved generalization across languages.
- **Strong Empirical Evidence**: Results consistently show the superiority of the proposed method over existing approaches, with notable reductions in language bias.
- **Generalizability**: The method demonstrates strong zero-shot performance on unseen low-resource languages like Greek (el) and Vietnamese (vi).

### Weaknesses:
- **Limitations in Scope**: The experiments are confined to specific datasets, potentially limiting generalizability to real-world scenarios. Additionally, the study does not address aspects such as result diversity or system transparency.
- **Ethical Considerations**: While societal biases in training data are acknowledged, the paper does not explore mitigation strategies comprehensively.

### Conclusion:
The paper makes a significant contribution to multilingual IR by presenting an effective and straightforward hybrid batch training strategy. It provides robust empirical evidence of its approach's benefits while highlighting important directions for future research, such as addressing ethical considerations and expanding dataset coverage.
