 The work presented in this paper introduces a novel hybrid batch training strategy designed to optimize Information Retrieval (IR) systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously. By fine-tuning multilingual language models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs, the models learn robust representations that generalize well across languages and retrieval settings.

### Key Contributions:
1. **Hybrid Batch Training Strategy**: The proposed approach combines both monolingual and cross-lingual data to train multilingual language models effectively.
2. **Improved Generalization**: Models trained with this strategy demonstrate superior generalizability across different languages, particularly in low-resource languages such as Vietnamese (vi) and Greek (el).
3. **Reduced Language Bias**: The hybrid training approach significantly mitigates language bias, enhancing the fairness of multilingual retrieval systems.

### Experimental Results:
- **Performance Metrics**: Comprehensive experiments were conducted on the XQuAD-R and MLQA-R datasets using fine-tuned XLM-R and LaBSE models under different training batch types (X-X-mono, X-Y, and Proposed).
- **MAP and Recall Scores**: The proposed method consistently matches or outperforms models trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data in various retrieval settings.
  - **XQuAD-R Dataset (LaBSE Model)**: Mean rank distance for the proposed method is 286.6, significantly lower than X-X-mono (410.2) and competitive with X-Y (295.4).
  - **MLQA-R Dataset (LaBSE Model)**: Mean rank distance for the proposed method is 195, slightly higher than X-Y (198.3), but still demonstrates strong performance in reducing language bias.

### Conclusion:
The hybrid batch training strategy introduced here successfully optimizes IR systems for multilingual retrieval tasks. The approach not only improves overall performance but also substantially mitigates the language bias present in traditional training methods. This work represents a significant step forward in developing equitable and effective multilingual retrieval systems.

### Limitations:
While this work focuses on optimizing retrieval performance, it does not address issues related to result diversity, fairness, or transparency in multilingual settings. Additionally, the experiments are limited to specific benchmark datasets (XQuAD-R, MLQA-R), and the robustness of these results across different domains and question types is an exciting future direction for research.
