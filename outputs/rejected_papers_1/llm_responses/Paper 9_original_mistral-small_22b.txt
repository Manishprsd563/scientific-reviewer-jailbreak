 Based on the provided paper, here's a summary of the key points:

**Problem:** Test Time Adaptation (TTA) methods can be computationally intensive, making them unsuitable for resource-constrained edge devices.

**Solution: Sparse TTA (STTA)** framework called SNAP-TTA that significantly reduces latency while maintaining competitive accuracy.

**Key Components of SNAP-TTA:**
1. **Class and Domain Representative Memory (CnDRM):** Selects representative samples from the test set based on class diversity and domain relevance.
2. **Inference Optimization with IoBMN:** Adapts normalization layers using representative samples to account for domain shifts, reducing inference latency.

**Experiments and Results:**
- SNAP-TTA consistently outperforms baseline methods under sparse adaptation settings.
- It achieves accuracy gains over the original counterparts in certain scenarios.
- Ablative studies show that using CnDRM and IoBMN together enhances performance in low-latency STTA scenarios.
- SNAP-TTA is effective regardless of model (CNN or ViT) and normalization types (BN, LN).

**Limitations and Future Work:**
- Dynamic adaptation rates based on data distribution changes could be explored.
- Adaptive confidence thresholds in CnDRM for improved sampling efficiency.
- Optimizing SNAP-TTA for both latency and memory is a potential avenue for future research.
