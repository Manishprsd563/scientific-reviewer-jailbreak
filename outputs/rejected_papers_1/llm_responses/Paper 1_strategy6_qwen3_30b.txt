**Novelty / Originality**: 5/5

- This is not a simple incremental improvement but a fundamentally different approach to addressing language bias in multilingual retrieval.
- The method is clearly distinct from existing approaches, as evidenced by the comparative results.

**Significance / Impact / Usefulness**: 5/5
- The paper addresses a critical problem in the field: language bias that hinders multilingual retrieval performance.
- The approach has clear potential impact for real-world multilingual IR systems.
- The results demonstrate consistent improvements across multiple datasets and models, supporting the claim of significant practical utility.

**Technical Soundness / Correctness**: 5/5
- The methodology is well-justified and theoretically sound.
- The paper clearly explains the technical approach: fine-tuning multilingual language models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs.
- The experimental design is appropriate for the research questions.

- The paper provides comprehensive validation with multiple datasets (XQuAD-R, MLQA-R), multiple models (XLM-R, LaBSE), and multiple metrics (MAP, Recall@1, rank distance).

- The results consistently support the paper's claims across all evaluation metrics.

**Reproducibility / Transparency**: 5/5
- The paper includes detailed tables with all experimental results.
- The methodology is described with sufficient precision for replication.
- The paper acknowledges limitations and provides context for the results.

**Related Work & Positioning**: 5/5

- The literature review appears comprehensive and well-structured.

**Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety**: 5/5

The paper represents a strong contribution to the field of multilingual information retrieval.
