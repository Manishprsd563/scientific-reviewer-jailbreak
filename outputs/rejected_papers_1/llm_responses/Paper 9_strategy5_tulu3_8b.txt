In conclusion, the proposed SNAP-TTA framework addresses a critical yet often overlooked aspect of transfer learning methodologies—namely, adaptation latency in resource-constrained environments such as edge devices. By leveraging sparse updates with carefully selected domain-representative samples and optimized normalization layers for inference efficiency, SNAP-TTA significantly enhances the feasibility and practicality of utilizing Transfer Learning Techniques (TTA) in real-time applications.

The comprehensive experimental validation across various benchmarks, datasets, and model architectures demonstrates the robustness and adaptability of SNAP-TTA. This approach not only achieves substantial reductions in latency but also preserves or even improves upon the classification accuracy compared to conventional TTA methods. The ablative studies further illuminate the contributions of each component within SNAP-TTA, underscoring their synergistic effects.

SNAP-TTA’s ability to integrate seamlessly with existing techniques (e.g., SAR and EATA) offers a versatile solution for practitioners seeking to optimize their models for both speed and accuracy under resource constraints. By reducing the computational burden associated with frequent model updates, SNAP-TTA makes TTA more accessible and applicable in settings where real-time decision-making is critical.

While this work has made significant strides towards advancing sparse adaptation techniques for TTAs, there remains room for further innovation and exploration.
