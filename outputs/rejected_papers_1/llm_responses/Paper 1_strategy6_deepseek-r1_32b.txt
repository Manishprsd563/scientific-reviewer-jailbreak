The paper presents a novel approach to enhancing multilingual information retrieval (IR) through a hybrid batch training strategy. This method combines monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs during model training, aiming to create robust representations that generalize well across languages. The study demonstrates that this approach consistently outperforms models trained with either monolingual or cross-lingual data alone, particularly in reducing language bias and achieving strong performance on unseen languages.

Key findings include:

1. **Reduced Language Bias**: The hybrid method significantly decreases language bias compared to monolingual training, ensuring fair treatment of all languages.
2. **Zero-Shot Generalization**: The model effectively handles languages not explicitly trained on, enhancing scalability.
3. **Comprehensive Testing**: Results are validated across multiple datasets (XQuAD-R, MLQA-R, MIRACL), showing consistent improvements in retrieval metrics like MAP and Recall.

Limitations acknowledged include the need for further exploration of fairness and transparency issues, as well as testing beyond current benchmark datasets to ensure broader applicability.

In conclusion, the hybrid training strategy offers a promising solution for developing more inclusive and effective multilingual IR systems.
