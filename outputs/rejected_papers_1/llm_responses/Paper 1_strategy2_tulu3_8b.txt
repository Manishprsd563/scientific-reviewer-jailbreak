This research paper presents a novel approach to improve multilingual information retrieval (IR) systems by introducing a hybrid batch training strategy that optimizes models for both monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual scenarios simultaneously. The proposed method fine-tunes the pre-trained language models on a combination of question-answer pairs from multiple languages in both formats.

**Novelty / Originality:**
The paper introduces a unique and novel technique to address the challenge of building robust IR systems that perform well across various languages without having to train separate models for each language or scenario. The concept of mixing monolingual and cross-lingual data during fine-tuning is an original approach within the field.

**Significance / Impact / Usefulness:**
This method has a significant potential impact, as it promises to improve the accessibility and effectiveness of information retrieval services in multilingual settings—benefiting users who interact with systems in multiple languages. Additionally, this work contributes to advancing academic understanding and practical utility in natural language processing (NLP) for multilingual applications.

**Technical Soundness / Correctness:**
The theoretical framework presented is technically sound and well-justified; the methodology follows a logical progression and seems rigorously executed. The authors provide a clear rationale for why their hybrid approach should work, which is supported by relevant literature.

**Empirical / Experimental Validation:**
The experiments are comprehensive, covering multiple datasets, pre-trained models (XLM-R and LaBSE), and languages (covering both resource-rich and low-resource languages). Results are reported in a detailed manner that supports the claims made within the paper. The authors show strong performance across different metrics (MAP, R@1, R@10) and settings (monolingual, cross-lingual, multilingual).
