 ## Research Paper: Hybrid Batch Training for Multilingual Information Retrieval Systems

### Abstract
Developing information retrieval (IR) models capable of handling queries and documents across multiple languages is becoming increasingly critical. In this work, we introduce a hybrid batch training strategy to optimize IR systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously. By fine-tuning multilingual language models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs, the models learn robust representations that generalize well across languages and retrieval settings. Extensive experiments demonstrate that this simple yet effective approach consistently matches or outperforms models trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data, and substantially mitigates language bias hindering multilingual retrieval performance.

### 1. Introduction
The increasing globalization of information has led to a significant demand for IR systems capable of handling multiple languages effectively. However, the current state-of-the-art IR models often struggle with multilingual queries and documents. This paper aims to address this challenge by introducing a hybrid batch training strategy that optimizes IR systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously.

### 2. Methodology
#### 2.1 Hybrid Batch Training Strategy
We propose a hybrid batch training approach where the training data includes both monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs. This strategy helps the model learn robust representations that can generalize across different languages and retrieval settings.

#### 2.2 Experimental Setup
The experiments were conducted using two popular multilingual pre-trained models: XLM-R and LaBSE. The evaluation datasets used include XQuAD-R and MLQA-R, covering a range of languages.

### 3. Results
#### 3.1 Retrieval Performance
The proposed approach exhibited remarkable zero-shot generalization to unseen languages such as Greek (el) and Vietnamese (vi), achieving strong performance across different multilingual pre-trained models and evaluation datasets. For example, in Table 5, which presents results for the LaBSE model on the XQuAD-R dataset, the proposed method achieves the best MAP and Recall @1 scores for Vietnamese, a low-resource language, outperforming both X-X-mono and X-Y approaches.

#### 3.2 Language Bias Mitigation
The proposed approach significantly mitigates language bias compared to monolingual batching. For instance, it reduces the mean rank distance by 30.1% for XLM-R and 15.4% for LaBSE on the XQuAD-R dataset. Additionally, it exhibits competitive performance in reducing the average rank distance compared to the strong X-Y baseline.

### 4. Discussion
#### 4.1 Zero-Shot Generalization
The proposed approach demonstrates remarkable zero-shot generalizability, outperforming models primarily optimized for monolingual retrieval and matching those optimized for multilingual retrieval. This highlights the effectiveness of the hybrid batch training strategy in learning robust cross-lingual representations.

#### 4.2 Language Bias Evaluation
Tables 7 and 8 present a comprehensive comparison of the average rank distance metric across different multilingual retrieval tasks using fine-tuned XLM-R and LaBSE models. The proposed approach achieves significant reductions in language bias compared to both monolingual and cross-lingual batching baselines, highlighting its effectiveness in mitigating language bias.

### 5. Conclusion
Developing IR models that can handle queries and documents across many languages is increasingly critical. In this work, we introduced a hybrid batch training strategy to optimize IR systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously. Extensive experiments demonstrate that this simple yet effective approach consistently matches or outperforms models trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data, and substantially mitigates the language bias hindering multilingual retrieval performance.

### 6. Limitations
This work focuses on optimizing retrieval performance but does not address issues related to result diversity, fairness, or transparency in multilingual settings. Additionally, the experiments focus only on specific benchmark datasets, and the robustness of the results to other domains and question types is an exciting future direction.
