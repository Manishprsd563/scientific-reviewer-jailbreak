**Overall Summary**

The paper presents a novel and effective training strategy – a “hybrid batch training” – to improve multilingual information retrieval (IR) systems. The core idea is to train models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual data, allowing them to perform well across different languages and retrieval scenarios. The results demonstrate that this simple yet effective approach consistently outperforms systems trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data, and significantly reduces language bias.

**Key Strengths**

*   **Clear Problem Statement:** The paper clearly articulates the challenges of building effective multilingual IR systems, particularly related to performance across languages and the presence of language bias.
*   **Novelty:** The proposed hybrid training strategy is a simple but effective way to address the challenges of multilingual IR. It’s not necessarily a revolutionary architectural change but a smart training modification.
*   **Strong Experimental Validation:** The paper includes extensive experiments across multiple datasets (XQuAD-R, MLQA-R, MIRACL) and with different models (XLM-R and LaBSE). This provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
*   **Thorough Analysis:** The paper provides a detailed analysis of the results, including comparisons to baseline methods and an evaluation of language bias using the average rank distance metric.
*   **Well-Structured and Readable:** The paper is well-organized, with clear headings and subheadings, making it easy to follow the main ideas and results.
* **Comprehensive Tables:** The inclusion of detailed tables with experimental results (including average rank distance) enhances the credibility and transparency of the study.

**Potential Areas for Improvement**

*   **Discussion of Limitations:** While the paper briefly mentions limitations, a more in-depth discussion would be valuable. For instance, exploring how the proposed approach might be affected by different types of language pairs (e.g., high-resource to low-resource) or different IR tasks (e.g., question answering, document retrieval).
*   **Ablation Studies:** Performing ablation studies could help to better understand the contribution of each component of the proposed approach. For example, investigating the effect of different ratios of monolingual and cross-lingual data in the training batch.
*   **Statistical Significance:** Providing statistical significance tests (e.g., t-tests, ANOVA) for the experimental results would strengthen the conclusions.
*   **Error Analysis:** Conducting an error analysis could help to identify the types of queries or documents that the proposed approach struggles with, and guide future research.
*   **Real-World Applications:** Expanding on the potential real-world applications of the proposed approach could increase its impact. For example, discussing how it could be used to improve search engines, machine translation systems, or cross-lingual information access.
*   **Diversity and Fairness:** The paper touches on fairness, but could benefit from a more detailed discussion of how the proposed approach might affect different language communities or cultural groups.

**Key Contributions**

1.  **Hybrid Batch Training Strategy:** The main contribution is the introduction of a novel training strategy that combines monolingual and cross-lingual data to improve multilingual IR performance.
2.  **Reduced Language Bias:** The paper demonstrates that the proposed approach effectively reduces language bias, leading to more fair and accurate retrieval results.
3.  **Strong Empirical Validation:** The paper provides extensive experimental evidence to support the effectiveness of the proposed approach across multiple datasets and with different models.
