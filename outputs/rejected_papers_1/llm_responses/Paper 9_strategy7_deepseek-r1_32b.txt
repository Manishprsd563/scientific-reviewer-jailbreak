**SNAP-TTA: Enhancing Test-Time Adaptation Efficiency for Edge Devices**

**Introduction:**
Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) aims to improve model performance during testing by adapting to domain shifts between training and test data. However, traditional TTA methods require frequent updates, leading to high latency, which is impractical for resource-constrained edge devices. SNAP-TTA addresses this by introducing a sparse update framework that reduces latency while maintaining accuracy.

**Key Components:**
1. **CnDRM (Class and Domain Representative Memory):**
   - Selects representative samples based on two criteria:
     - **Class-Representative:** High confidence predictions to ensure sample correctness.
     - **Domain-Representative:** Samples close to the target domain in feature space for relevance.

2. **IoBMN (Inference-Optimized Batch Normalization):**
   - Adapts normalization layers using stored representative samples' statistics, adjusted slightly with the current batch to avoid drastic changes. This method may involve an exponential moving average (EMA) to gradually update memory statistics.

**Experimental Results:**
- SNAP-TTA significantly reduces latency by up to 87.5% while maintaining or improving accuracy compared to traditional methods.
- Tested on various datasets (CIFAR10-C, CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C) and models (CNNs and Vision Transformers), demonstrating versatility.

**Mechanisms:**
- **Domain-Representative Selection:** Involves computing feature similarity metrics between source and target domains to identify relevant samples.
- **IoBMN Functionality:** Combines stored statistics with current batch data using EMA, allowing gradual adaptation without abrupt changes.

**Ablation Studies:**
- Results show that both CnDRM and IoBMN are crucial, working synergistically to enhance performance in low-latency scenarios.

**Considerations and Limitations:**
- **Confidence Threshold:** Currently fixed but could be dynamically adjusted for flexibility.
- **Memory Management:** Efficiently stores representative samples with minimal overhead, possibly using indices rather than feature vectors.
- **Domain Shift Handling:** Performs well across gradual and abrupt shifts, particularly effective with higher adaptation rates.

**Future Directions:**
- Dynamic threshold adjustment and continuous domain adaptation for real-world scenarios.
- Generalization to other architectures and normalization techniques.

**Conclusion:**
SNAP-TTA offers a robust solution for efficient TTA on edge devices by combining smart sampling and adaptive normalization. The approach is validated across diverse datasets and models, highlighting its potential for practical applications in resource-constrained environments.
