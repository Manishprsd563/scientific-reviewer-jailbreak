**Paper Summary – “SNAP‑TTA: Reducing Latency of Test‑Time Adaptation for Edge Devices”**

- **Problem Addressed:**  
  Test‑time adaptation (TTA) improves model robustness on unseen domains, but many state‑of‑the‑art (SOTA) TTA methods suffer high inference latency, especially problematic for low‑power edge devices. Existing work has focused on accuracy, not latency or memory usage.

- **Proposed Solution – SNAP‑TTA:**  
  1. **Sparse Test‑Time Adaptation (STTA):**  
     Adaptation is performed only at a fraction of inference steps, governed by an *adaptation rate* (AR).  
  2. **Class‑Domain Representative Memory (CnDRM):**  
     - Samples for adaptation are chosen based on *class‑representativeness* (high pseudo‑label confidence) and *domain‑representativeness* (proximity to the domain centre in feature space).  
     - This dual sampling yields more informative update data while keeping the number of samples small.  
  3. **Inference‑Time Batch‑Normalization Modification (IoBMN):**  
     - Normalization statistics are derived from the representative memory and corrected to match the current test batch, reducing domain‑shift impact without full batch‑norm recalculation.

- **Key Findings:**  
  - When applied to 7 SOTA TTA methods (Tent, CoTTA, EATA, SAR, RoTTA, etc.), SNAP‑TTA cuts average latency per batch by up to **87 %** on Raspberry Pi 4, while preserving or improving classification accuracy across CIFAR10/100‑C and ImageNet‑C datasets.  
  - Performance drops that SOTA TTA suffer under sparse updates are largely mitigated; in some cases SNAP‑TTA even outperforms the fully adapted baseline.  
  - The two core components (CnDRM + IoBMN) are critical; ablative studies show that domain‑representative sampling and memory‑guided normalization give the largest gains.  
  - SNAP‑TTA is lightweight: it adds negligible memory overhead and is compatible with memory‑efficient TTA schemes such as MECTA.

- **Limitations & Future Directions:**  
  - Dynamic adjustment of the adaptation rate or confidence threshold could further optimise streaming scenarios.  
  - Extending the framework to continuous domain‑shift streams and other normalisation types (e.g., LayerNorm in ViT) is a promising avenue.

- **Conclusion:**  
  SNAP‑TTA offers a general, low‑latency augmentation that can be plugged into existing TTA pipelines, enabling robust, real‑time adaptation on edge devices without sacrificing accuracy.
