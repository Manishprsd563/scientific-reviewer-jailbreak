**Title:** SNAP‑TTA: Improving the Latency of Test Time Adaptation with Class‑Domain Representative Memory and Adaptive Batch Normalization  
**Overall Score:** 32/40  

---

### Summary
The authors identify a neglected practical bottleneck—runtime latency—of test‑time adaptation (TTA) methods on edge devices. They propose **SNAP‑TTA**, a modular framework that turns any existing TTA algorithm into a *Sparse Test‑Time Adaptation* (STTA) pipeline. STTA reduces the frequency of parameter updates by selecting a small, high‑value set of **class‑domain representative (CnDRM)** samples for adaptation. For inference, they introduce **Inferred Batch‑Normalization with Memory‑Narrowing (IoBMN)**, a lightweight technique that corrects batch‑norm statistics using the representative memory. Extensive experiments on CIFAR‑10/100‑C and ImageNet‑C, across five state‑of‑the‑art baselines, demonstrate up to 87 % reduction in per‑batch latency on a Raspberry‑Pi 4 while retaining or improving classification accuracy, even at very low adaptation rates (0.01–0.5). Ablations confirm the distinct contributions of CnDRM and IoBMN. The method is also shown to be compatible with Vision‑Transformer back‑bones and with memory‑efficient TTA variants.

---

### Strengths
1. **Novelty & Technical Soundness (4/5):** The idea of coupling class‑domain representative sampling with adaptive normalization for STTA is novel and logically coherent. The mathematical formulation of the adaptation rate and memory update policies is clear.  
2. **Empirical Breadth (4/5):** Experiments cover three benchmark corruptions, five baselines, and multiple adaptation rates, providing convincing evidence of the method’s practicality.  
3. **Reproducibility (3/5):** The manuscript cites datasets and baselines, but concrete code, hyper‑parameter settings, and detailed training scripts are not publicly linked. The claim of “negligible memory overhead” is only referenced in appendices that are not fully available.  
4. **Impact (4/5):** Latency reduction is a pressing issue for real‑time vision on resource‑constrained hardware; thus the proposed approach has tangible industry relevance.  
5. **Positioning (4/5):** The related‑work section situates SNAP‑TTA well relative to data‑efficient TTA, active‑learning sampling, and adaptive batch‑norm techniques, clarifying its contributions.

---

### Weaknesses & Recommendations
- **Clarity & Writing:** The manuscript suffers from typographical errors, inconsistent formatting, and occasional ambiguous terminology (“class‑domain representative memory” is sometimes described as a single component, other times as two). A thorough copy‑editing would improve readability.  
- **Theoretical Justification:** While the empirical gains are evident, a more rigorous analysis of why CnDRM and IoBMN synergize under sparse updates would strengthen the contribution.  
- **Generalizability:** Experiments are limited to static corruption datasets. A demonstration on a continuous domain‑shift scenario or an online streaming benchmark would broaden the appeal.  
- **Ethics & Broader Impact:** The paper briefly notes licensing and safety concerns but lacks a detailed discussion of potential misuse or bias in the sampled memory.  
- **Reproducibility:** Providing a public repository with scripts, hyper‑parameters, and a reproducibility checklist would meet the community’s standards.

---

### Recommendation
The manuscript addresses a significant, practical problem with a conceptually novel solution. The experimental evidence is strong, yet the presentation and reproducibility aspects need refinement. **I recommend acceptance with major revisions**: tighten the narrative, improve clarity, provide full code and hyper‑parameters, and extend evaluations to streaming scenarios. This would elevate the paper to the conference’s flagship standard while preserving its innovative contribution.
