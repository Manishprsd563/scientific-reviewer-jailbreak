**Incorporating Latency-Conscious Adaptation in Time-Series Data Stream Processing**

*Abstract:* As machine learning (ML) models are increasingly deployed on resource-constrained edge devices, the need for efficient model adaptation becomes critical. In this context, we revisit the adaptation process of time-series models used for stream processing applications, such as anomaly detection or predictive maintenance. Traditional techniques focus primarily on improving accuracy at the cost of computational efficiency and latency. This paper proposes a latency-conscious adaptation framework specifically designed for stream-processing scenarios, leveraging insights from recent work in Transfer Learning (TL) and Sparse Adaptation Techniques (SAT).

*Introduction:* The deployment of ML models on edge devices often requires real-time adaptability to handle concept drifts – changes in the statistical properties of the data over time. Time-series data, particularly prevalent in IoT applications, poses unique challenges due to their sequential nature and continuous generation. Existing adaptation methods, while effective at improving model performance, often introduce unacceptable latencies, especially in critical applications such as healthcare monitoring or industrial control systems.

*Methodology:* Our framework, named "StreamLite Adapt", integrates Sparse Transfer Learning (STL) principles with a novel latency management strategy. At the core of StreamLite Adapt is:

1. **Sparse Sampling for Efficient Updates:** Employing sparse sampling techniques to reduce the frequency and volume of data used for adaptation.
2. **Latency-Aware Normalization Layer Adaptation:** Optimizing normalization layers using minimal yet representative samples, minimizing the computational overhead.
3. **Adaptive Batch Processing:** Dynamically adjusting batch sizes based on current system load and application requirements.

*Experimental Evaluation:* We evaluate StreamLite Adapt on several time-series datasets, including synthetic data designed to mimic real-world edge device scenarios. Key metrics include prediction accuracy, latency, and energy consumption. Compared with traditional full-batch adaptation methods, StreamLite Adapt shows substantial improvements in both latency (up to 70% reduction) and computational efficiency while maintaining competitive predictive performance.

*Discussion:* The challenges of balancing model adaptability and latency are underscored by real-world considerations such as intermittent connectivity, limited compute resources, and stringent response times. Our approach aims to mitigate these issues without compromising the accuracy gains that adaptation brings.

*Conclusion:* StreamLite Adapt offers a promising direction for designing adaptive ML models suitable for edge devices handling time-series data streams. By prioritizing low-latency adaptations, it opens new avenues for applying complex models in scenarios previously constrained by computational and timing limits.

**Incorporating Latency-Conscious Adaptation into Autonomous Vehicles' Perception Systems**

*Abstract:* In autonomous vehicle (AV) perception systems, the continuous adaptation of machine learning models to changing traffic conditions is crucial. However, conventional adaptation strategies often introduce unacceptable latencies due to their high computational demands, which could pose safety risks. This paper introduces "Perceptual Adapt Lite," a latency-conscious adaptation framework designed specifically for AV perception tasks such as object detection and scene understanding.

*Introduction:* Autonomous vehicles' perception systems must adapt quickly and safely to changing environments, from varying weather conditions to dynamic traffic patterns. However, current approaches that aim to enhance model accuracy often result in significant latencies and increased computational burdens, potentially leading to safety-critical situations.

*Methodology:* Perceptual Adapt Lite integrates Sparse Adaptive Inference (SAI) techniques with latency-sensitive modifications:

1. **Sparse Adaptation Triggering:** Utilizing sparse data sampling and anomaly detection to intelligently trigger adaptation only when necessary.
2. **Efficient Normalization Layer Updates:** Employing a lightweight, low-latency method for updating normalization layers during adaptation.
3. **Resource Management Policies:** Dynamic resource allocation policies that balance between adaptation frequency, model accuracy, and response time.

*Experimental Evaluation:* We evaluate Perceptual Adapt Lite in simulation environments replicating urban driving conditions, focusing on latency metrics, safety (measured through reaction times), and model accuracy. Results indicate up to a 50% reduction in adaptation latency while maintaining high detection rates for critical objects.

*Discussion:* The challenge lies in ensuring that the trade-offs between adaptability, computational efficiency, and safety are carefully managed within the strict operational constraints of autonomous vehicles. Our approach emphasizes the importance of real-time adaptability without compromising on latency.

*Conclusion:* Perceptual Adapt Lite represents a step forward in deploying adaptive ML models for AV perception systems, prioritizing low-latency adaptations to enhance safety while maintaining high model accuracy levels. This framework sets a precedent for future research in integrating efficient adaptation mechanisms within critical applications like autonomous driving.
