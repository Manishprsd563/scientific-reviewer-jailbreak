This paper presents a hybrid batch training strategy to optimize information retrieval (IR) systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously. The proposed approach fine-tunes multilingual language models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs, enabling the models to learn robust representations that generalize well across languages and retrieval settings.

The paper's main contributions are:

1.  **Hybrid Batch Training Strategy**: The authors propose a hybrid batch training strategy that combines both monolingual and cross-lingual data in each mini-batch.
2.  **Robust Representations**: By fine-tuning multilingual language models on a mix of monolingual and cross-lingual question-answer pairs, the models learn robust representations that generalize well across languages and retrieval settings.
3.  **Mitigation of Language Bias**: The proposed approach substantially reduces language bias compared to the X-X-mono baseline.

The paper conducts extensive experiments using two datasets: XQuAD-R and MLQA-R. The results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently matches or outperforms models trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data in both monolingual and multilingual retrieval settings.

**Key Results**

1.  **Robust Representations**: The hybrid batch training strategy enables the models to learn robust representations that generalize well across languages and retrieval settings.
2.  **Mitigation of Language Bias**: The proposed approach substantially reduces language bias compared to the X-X-mono baseline, with a reduction of up to 30.1% in the average rank distance metric.
3.  **Performance Improvement**: The proposed method consistently matches or outperforms models trained with only monolingual or cross-lingual data in both monolingual and multilingual retrieval settings.

**Limitations**

1.  **Dataset Bias**: While the XQuAD-R dataset is considered a 'gold standard' for benchmarking novel architectures, it has known distributional biases.
2.  **Generalizability**: The results are based on two specific datasets (XQuAD-R and MLQA-R) and might not generalize to other domains or question types.

**Future Work**

1.  **Addressing Dataset Bias**: Investigating methods to mitigate the dataset bias and develop more representative benchmarks for multilingual IR.
2.  **Exploring Other Architectures**: Evaluating the performance of different architectures, such as transformers or BERT-based models, with the proposed hybrid batch training strategy.

Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of multilingual information retrieval by introducing a hybrid batch training strategy that optimizes retrieval systems for monolingual, cross-lingual, and multilingual performance simultaneously.
