# AGPD: Activation Gradient-based Poisoned Sample Detection for Backdoor Attacks

This paper introduces AGPD (Activation Gradient-based Poisoned Sample Detection), a novel method for detecting backdoor attacks in machine learning models. The key innovation lies in leveraging the "gradient circular distribution" (GCD) concept to identify poisoned samples.

## Core Methodology

AGPD operates in three main stages:

1. **Identify the target class**: Using a Circular Variance-Based Test (CVBT) metric to determine which class is the target of the backdoor attack.

2. **Identify the farthest basis**: For the target class, find the activation gradient that is farthest from clean samples.

3. **Filter poisoned samples**: Use a stopping criteria based on JS divergence to determine the optimal iteration for filtering out poisoned samples.

## Key Innovations

1. **Activation gradients vs. activation spaces**: The authors demonstrate that activation gradients provide better separation between poisoned and clean samples than activation spaces. This is quantified using Silhouette Scores (higher scores indicate better separation).

2. **CVBT vs. variance**: Their Circular Variance-Based Test (CVBT) metric outperforms simple variance in identifying the target class. For example:
   - BadNets: CVBT Z-score = 21.34 vs. Variance Z-score = 15.53
   - LF: CVBT Z-score = 24.74 vs. Variance Z-score = 12.90

3. **Optimal stopping criteria**: The authors use a sliding window method with JS divergence to determine the optimal iteration for filtering poisoned samples.

## Performance Results

AGPD significantly outperforms existing methods across multiple metrics:

| Dataset | Model | AGPD TPR | Runner-up TPR | AGPD FPR | Runner-up FPR |
|---------|-------|----------|---------------|----------|---------------|
| CIFAR-10 | VGG19-BN | 96.46% | 80.00% | 1.87% | 16.55% |
| Tiny ImageNet | VGG19-BN | 97.75% | 90.00% | 0.09% | 7.89% |

On CIFAR-10 with VGG19-BN:
- AGPD achieved **96.46% TPR** (True Positive Rate)
- AGPD achieved **1.87% FPR** (False Positive Rate)
- AGPD achieved **90.90% F1 score** (vs. runner-up's 57.31%)

## Key Evidence Supporting the Method

1. **Gradient circular distribution (GCD)**: The target class (poisoned samples) occupies a longer arc on the circle compared to clean classes (see Figure 13-14).

2. **Better separation**: Activation gradient space shows significantly better separation between clean and poisoned samples than activation space (see Figure 15 and Table 16).

3. **Robustness**: The method works across:
   - Different model architectures (Preact-ResNet18, VGG19-BN)
   - Different poisoning ratios (1-10%)
   - Different attack types (BadNets, Blended, LF, SSBA, WaNet, etc.)
   - Different datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet)

## Computational Efficiency

AGPD has a computational complexity of O((F + B + D)N), which is comparable to existing methods and negligible compared to model training costs.

## Conclusion

AGPD provides a highly effective method for detecting backdoor attacks by leveraging the circular nature of activation gradients. The method outperforms all baselines across multiple datasets, attack types, and model architectures while maintaining low false positive rates. The authors convincingly demonstrate that activation gradients provide better separation between poisoned and clean samples than activation spaces, which is the key innovation behind their approach.
