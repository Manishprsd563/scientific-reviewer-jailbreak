We've covered a broad range of topics and experiments related to the paper "A Scalable Constructive Model with Self-Improved Training for Large-Scale Vehicle Routing Problems." Here's a summary of key points and discussions:

1. **Paper Overview**: The paper introduces a scalable constructive model equipped with Self-Improved Training (SIT) for solving large-scale Vehicle Routing Problems (VRPs). It addresses the limitations of existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Supervised Learning (SL) methods by enabling direct training on large problem instances.

2. **Key Innovation**: The proposed cross-attention model ensures linear complexity, which improves inference efficiency significantly, especially for large-scale problems. The SIT strategy allows for the generation of high-quality pseudo-labels, enhancing the model's performance on large-scale VRP instances.

3. **Experiments and Results**:
   - **Synthetic Instances**: The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across various problem scales, significantly outperforming other NCO methods.
   - **TSPLIB and CVRPLIB**: It shows competitive results against classical solvers and other recent methods, often achieving better solution quality in a shorter time.
   - **Large-Scale Instances Beyond 100K Nodes**: The model demonstrates its ability to scale and solve extremely large VRP instances, maintaining relatively low optimality gaps.

4. **Technical Details**:
   - **Cross-Attention**: This model structure is designed to reduce complexity while effectively capturing the necessary information for VRP problems.
   - **Self-Improved Training (SIT)**: A training strategy that leverages local reconstruction to iteratively improve the pseudo-labels and, consequently, the model's performance.

5. **Comparisons**:
   - **With Baseline Methods**: The proposed method significantly outperforms other baseline methods in terms of solution quality and computational efficiency.
   - **With Recently Published RL-Based Methods**: Despite their larger model sizes, the paper’s model still outperforms these recently published RL-based methods.

6. **Discussion on Limitations and Future Work**:
   - **Limitation**: Training models on very large-scale VRP instances can result in prohibitively long training times.
   - **Future Work**: Multi-GPU parallel training could be a coping strategy to address this issue, along with exploring more effective schemes for generating high-quality pseudo-labels.

7. **Open Access and Licensing**: The paper adheres to open access principles, ensuring the availability of codes and datasets under appropriate licenses that facilitate academic research use.

The paper's contributions lie in its scalable approach, innovative training strategy, and demonstrated ability to solve large-scale VRP instances efficiently. The experimental results underscore the potential of the proposed method in advancing the field of VRP solving through constructive model approaches.
