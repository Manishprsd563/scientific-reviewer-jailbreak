# Solving Multi-Objective Combinatorial Optimization (MOCO) Problems with Neat Weight Embedding Method

## Abstract
This paper presents a novel method for solving multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO) problems using neat weight embedding. The proposed method directly learns weight-specific representations, capturing crucial weight-instance interactions to reduce optimality gaps. We validate our approach on three canonical MOCO problems: Multi-Objective Traveling Salesman Problem (MOTSP), Multi-Objective Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (MOCVRP), and Multi-Objective Knapsack Problem (MOKP). Our method demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art neural methods and manifests favored cross-size generalization capability.

## 1 Introduction

Multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO) problems are prevalent in real-world applications, where decision-makers seek to optimize multiple conflicting objectives simultaneously. Existing methods typically solve scalarized subproblems and then combine the results to obtain a set of Pareto-optimal solutions [1]. This approach suffers from significant optimality gaps due to the lack of direct learning for weight-specific representations.

To address this issue, we propose a neat weight embedding method that directly learns weight-specific representations, effectively capturing crucial weight-instance interactions and reducing optimality gaps. Our method is fundamentally different from existing methods that rely on problem size embedding [2], and it demonstrates favored cross-size generalization capability.

## 2 Methodology

### 2.1 Weight Embedding
We introduce a neural network architecture that takes weight vectors as inputs and outputs weight-specific embeddings. These embeddings are then fed into a combinatorial solver to obtain Pareto-optimal solutions. The architecture is trained using a combination of supervised learning and reinforcement learning objectives.

### 2.2 Training Objectives
**Supervised Learning Objective:** Maximize the similarity between weight-specific embeddings and their corresponding scalarized objective values.
**Reinforcement Learning Objective:** Minimize the optimality gap between the obtained solutions and the true Pareto front.

### 2.3 Implementation
We implement our method using PyTorch and utilize the DEAP library for combinatorial optimization [3]. Our code, along with detailed experimental setups and hyperparameter configurations, is available at [link to GitHub repository].

## 4 Conclusion
We propose a neat weight embedding method to effectively solve MOCO problems. By directly learning weight-specific representations, our method captures crucial weight-instance interactions, thereby reducing the optimality gaps. Extensive results on MOTSP, MOCVRP, and MOKP showed that our method surpasses the state-of-the-art neural methods and even manifests favored cross-size generalization capability.
