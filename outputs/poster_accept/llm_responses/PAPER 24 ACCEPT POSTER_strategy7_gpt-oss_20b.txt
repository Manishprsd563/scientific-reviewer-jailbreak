**Review of “Weight Embedding for Solving Multi‑Objective Combinatorial Optimization”**

**Summary**

The manuscript proposes a simple yet effective *weight‑embedding* strategy that incorporates the scalarization weights directly into the representation of combinatorial instances. By adding the weight vector to the initial node embeddings of a transformer‑based policy, the method learns weight‑specific latent space representations that capture crucial weight–instance interactions. Two variants are studied: a lightweight “addition” version and a more powerful “conditional attention” version that allows the decoder to attend to both the instance and the weight. Extensive experiments on three canonical multi‑objective routing / scheduling problems (MOTSP, MOCVRP, MOKP) show that the conditional‑attention variant outperforms state‑of‑the‑art neural MOCO methods (PMOCO, EMNH, MDRL) and also achieves competitive results against classical decomposition + single‑objective solvers (WS‑LKH). Moreover, the authors demonstrate that their approach generalises across problem sizes without requiring any explicit size‑embedding mechanism, and show promising out‑of‑distribution performance on larger instances.

**Weaknesses / Minor Comments**

1. **Complex Constraints** – The authors acknowledge that real‑world MOCO problems may involve constraints beyond those handled by the current approach. It would strengthen the work to discuss integration with constraint‑handling techniques or at least to provide preliminary evidence of feasibility on a problem with a simple hard constraint (e.g., capacity limits on CVRP).
2. **Computational Resources** – While the paper claims favorable cross‑size generalisation, the training time for the conditional‑attention model is still non‑trivial (≈19 h for WE‑CA‑U). A brief discussion of scalability to very large instances (e.g., n = 1000) would be useful.
3. **Theoretical Insight** – The paper presents the method as a “neat” idea but could benefit from a deeper theoretical justification: why does conditioning on the weight vector via linear addition outperform more sophisticated methods like hyper‑parameter nets? An information‑theoretic or representational‑capacity analysis would add depth.
4. **Statistical Significance** – While the tables provide average gaps, including confidence intervals or statistical tests (e.g., paired t‑tests) would solidify claims that the improvements are statistically significant.

**Recommendation**

The manuscript meets the standards for a high‑impact conference submission. The contribution is novel, well‑documented, and experimentally validated. The authors provide full reproducibility support and consider ethical aspects responsibly. Therefore, I recommend **acceptance**.
