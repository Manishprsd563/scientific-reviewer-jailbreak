The paper introduces a novel approach for detecting poisoned samples in the context of defending against backdoor attacks in deep learning models. By analyzing the circular distribution of gradients with respect to sample activation (GCD), the authors identify two key observations: the GCD of samples in the target class is more dispersed than clean classes, and poisoned and clean samples within the target class are clearly separated in GCD. Based on these observations, the paper proposes a three-stage method called Activation Gradient based Poisoned sample Detection (AGPD) that first identifies the target class(es) and then filters out poisoned samples.

The evaluation of AGPD under various backdoor attacks demonstrates its superior performance compared to existing methods. The paper also provides an analysis of the effectiveness of AGPD against adaptive attacks, showcasing its robustness. The contributions of this work are significant in advancing the field of backdoor defense in deep learning models.

However, it is essential to note that while the approach presented in the paper shows promising results, the field of backdoor defense is continuously evolving with new attack and defense methods being developed. As such, ongoing research is necessary to ensure the robustness of defense mechanisms against both existing and future backdoor attacks.
