# Research Paper Review

## Novelty / Originality
The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various tabular data models, but its novelty is limited. While it introduces "TabM" (with variants like TabM[♠], TabM mini, and TabM[†]) and "Trompt" as new models, these appear to be incremental variations rather than fundamentally novel approaches. The paper doesn't clearly articulate what makes these models different from existing ones like ResNet, MLP-Mixer, or other neural network architectures for tabular data. The most significant contribution appears to be the comprehensive empirical comparison rather than a theoretical or architectural innovation.

## Significance / Impact / Usefulness
The paper provides a useful reference for practitioners by offering a thorough comparison of many models across numerous tabular datasets. This is valuable for practitioners trying to choose appropriate models for specific tasks. However, the paper doesn't address a significant gap in the field or provide clear guidance beyond the empirical results. The practical utility is somewhat limited as many of the models evaluated (like XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) are already well-established in the community.

## Technical Soundness / Correctness
The methodology appears technically sound, following standard practices for machine learning model evaluation. The paper uses appropriate metrics for each task (accuracy for classification, RMSE for regression), and the results are presented with standard deviations, indicating proper statistical reporting. The experimental design seems robust, with multiple random seeds reflected in the ± values. However, without seeing the exact architecture details of the proposed "TabM" and "Trompt" models, it's difficult to fully assess their technical soundness.

## Empirical / Experimental Validation
The empirical validation is extensive and well-executed. The paper evaluates models across 36 different datasets with varying tasks (classification, regression), providing both single-model and ensemble results. The results tables are clear and comprehensive, showing consistent performance patterns across different datasets. The evaluation covers a broad range of models, including traditional gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), neural network approaches (ResNet, MLP, SNN), and newer architectures (SAINT, T2G, etc.).

## Reproducibility / Transparency
The paper provides sufficient detail for reproduction in terms of results (with standard deviations), but lacks crucial information about code availability and dataset details. The paper doesn't mention whether the code or data used in the experiments will be made publicly available, which is essential for full reproducibility. The detailed results tables help with reproducibility to some extent, but without code, it would be challenging for others to replicate the exact implementation of the proposed models.

## Related Work & Positioning
The paper seems to position itself within the context of existing tabular data models, but without seeing the related work section, it's difficult to assess how well it situates its contributions. The paper appears to compare against established baselines (XGBoost, LightGBM, etc.) and other neural network approaches, but doesn't clearly articulate how its proposed models differ from or improve upon existing work in the field.

## Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety
The paper doesn't address ethical considerations, potential biases in the datasets, or broader societal impacts of the models being evaluated. This is common for machine learning performance papers, but it's worth noting as a limitation.

## Overall Assessment
This is a well-executed empirical study that provides a comprehensive comparison of tabular data models across numerous datasets. The paper's main contribution is in its thorough evaluation rather than in introducing novel methodologies or theoretical insights. While the results are valuable for practitioners, the paper doesn't make a significant theoretical contribution to the field.

**Recommendation**: Accept with minor revisions (if the authors can clarify the novelty of TabM and Trompt, and provide more detail on code/data availability for reproducibility).

**Strengths**: Comprehensive dataset coverage, clear presentation of results, standard statistical reporting.

**Weaknesses**: Limited novelty, insufficient detail on proposed models' architecture, missing code/data availability information.

**Overall Rating**: The paper makes a useful contribution to the field as a reference for model performance on tabular data, but its novelty is limited and it doesn't advance the state of the art significantly.
