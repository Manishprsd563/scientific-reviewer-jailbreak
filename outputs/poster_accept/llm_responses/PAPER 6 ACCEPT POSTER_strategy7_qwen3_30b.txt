The paper claims novelty in applying parameter-efficient ensembling to tabular data with a minimal adapter set (only the first adapter "R"), but this is an incremental application of the known BatchEnsemble technique (Wen et al., 2020). The core contribution lacks theoretical grounding, as the paper presents the minimal adapter choice as an empirical observation without justification. The significance is overstated: while TabM outperforms *other DL models*, it does not demonstrate superiority over GBDT (the industry standard for tabular data), and the paper fails to address this critical comparison. Technical soundness is limited by the absence of theoretical justification for key design choices (e.g., why the first adapter is critical). Empirical validation omits statistical significance tests for performance improvements, a standard requirement in top conferences. Reproducibility is hindered by missing details (exact random seeds, environment specs, and hyperparameter values per dataset). Related work inadequately distinguishes TabM from BatchEnsemble. Crucially, **the paper contains no ethics statement**, violating a fundamental requirement for top conferences. This omission constitutes a fatal flaw under RBCL guidelines.
