 ### Evaluation of the Research Paper "RASA: RANK-SHARING LOW-RANK ADAPTATION"

#### Introduction
1. **Problem Statement**: The paper addresses the limitation of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), specifically its low expressive capacity due to the rank constraint. LoRA is a prominent method for parameter-efficient fine-tuning but lags behind Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) in tasks requiring large training datasets and complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation.
2. **Solution**: The proposed solution, Rank-Sharing Low-Rank Adaptation (RaSA), enhances LoRA by allowing partial rank sharing across layers. This increases the effective rank of parameter updates without augmenting parameter overhead.
3. **Evaluation Metrics**: RaSA is evaluated based on its ability to improve matrix reconstruction and performance in downstream tasks like code generation and mathematical reasoning.

#### Methodology
1. **Formulation**: LoRA constrains weight update matrices $\Delta W$ to be low-rank, which limits expressive capacity. RaSA mitigates this by sharing ranks across layers. For a layer $i$, it takes out $k$ ranks from each layer’s LoRA update and forms shared rank pools $(B_S, A_S)$ across all layers with layer-specific weighting.
2. **Theoretical Ground**: RaSA increases the effective rank of $\Delta W$. The minimum reconstruction error for RaSA is theoretically proven to be bounded by that of LoRA.
3. **Empirical Validation**: Coordinate descent is used to demonstrate RaSA's lower reconstruction error compared to LoRA, especially when $k$ is relatively small.
4. **Experimental Setup**: Tasks include instruction fine-tuning for code generation and mathematical reasoning using challenging benchmarks like Humaneval+ and MATH. LLMs and training details follow common practice.

#### Main Results
1. **Task Performance**:
   - **Code Generation**: RaSA outperforms all LoRA variants in terms of Pass@1 and Pass@10, achieving 59.5% PASS@1 with Llama-3.1-8B.
   - **Mathematical Reasoning**: RaSA demonstrates consistent superiority over PEFT baselines across configurations. It achieves 31.4% ACC on MATH compared to LoRA’s 29.0%.

#### Scalability and Robustness
1. **Model Scaling**: Larger models like Llama-3.1-70B and Mistral-8x7B show enhanced performance with RaSA over LoRA, achieving MATH accuracies of 40.4% and 32.6%, respectively.
2. **Data Scaling**: RaSA's performance improves significantly with increased training data size compared to LoRA. It outperforms LoRA even when utilizing only 25% of the training data.

#### Related Work
1. **PEFT Methods**: Other methods include adapter-based and prompt-based fine-tuning, but LoRA is more efficient by reducing inference latency.
2. **Parameter Redundancy of LoRA**: Research shows that LoRA’s parameters are underutilized, with RaSA aiming to improve parameter utilization through interlayer rank sharing.

#### Conclusion
1. **Summary of Contributions**:
   - RaSA introduces an innovative extension to LoRA by allowing partial rank sharing across layers.
   - Theoretically and empirically demonstrated superior matrix reconstruction capabilities compared to traditional LoRA, leading to improved downstream task performance in high-demand scenarios.
2. **Future Work**: Further exploration of rank-sharing schemes and RaSA's potential in a broader range of applications is suggested.
