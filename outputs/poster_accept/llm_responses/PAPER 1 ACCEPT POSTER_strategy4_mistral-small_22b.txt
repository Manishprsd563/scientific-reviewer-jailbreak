 The paper presents a comprehensive theoretical analysis of dropout in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), revealing that its primary role differs fundamentally from standard neural networks - preventing oversmoothing rather than co-adaptation. This finding represents a significant shift in our understanding of how regularization operates in graph-structured data and bridges a critical gap in the theoretical foundation of GCNs. The theoretical framework is rigorous, with all assumptions well justified. The methodology is correctly implemented, providing new insights into dropout's role in mitigating oversmoothing and deriving novel generalization bounds that account for graph-specific dropout effects. The paper is validated through extensive experiments on both node-level and graph-level tasks across 14 datasets, demonstrating practical significance and consistency with theoretical predictions. These findings have the potential to advance the field of graph representation learning by suggesting more principled approaches to leveraging dropout in GCNs.
