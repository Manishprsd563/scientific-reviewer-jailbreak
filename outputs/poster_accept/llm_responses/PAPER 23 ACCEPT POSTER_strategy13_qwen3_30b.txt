### Review of "PROBABILISTIC CONFORMAL PREDICTION WITH APPROXIMATE CONDITIONAL VALIDITY"

#### **1. Novelty / Originality: 3/5**  
The paper proposes "CP²" (Conformal Prediction with Approximate Conditional Validity) as an extension of probabilistic conformal prediction (PCP) to achieve adaptive radius-based prediction sets. While the adaptive radius mechanism (pointwise adjustment using the conditional distribution estimator) is a logical extension, the novelty is **not clearly differentiated** from existing PCP (Wang et al., 2023), which the authors cite as a baseline. The paper claims CP² generalizes PCP but fails to:  
- Explicitly distinguish *how* the adaptive radius differs from PCP’s fixed-radius approach in prior work.  
- Cite or contrast with prior attempts at adaptive conformal prediction (e.g., localized methods like LCR or CPCG).  
- Demonstrate that the adaptive radius alone drives the performance gains (experiments conflate radius adaptation with other methodological choices).  
**Weakness**: The core idea is incremental rather than transformative. Theoretical novelty is limited to a slight refinement of PCP’s framework, not a fundamentally new paradigm.  

---

#### **2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness: 3/5**  
The method addresses a valid problem: marginal coverage is insufficient for heteroscedastic data, and conditional coverage is crucial. Experiments show CP²-PCP achieves better conditional coverage than PCP on 7/10 real-world datasets (e.g., 0.85–0.95 vs. 0.9 nominal). However:  
- **Critical limitation**: The method *requires a high-quality conditional distribution estimator* (e.g., MDN). Without it, theoretical guarantees degrade (Theorem 3.2 depends on total variation distance). The paper does not discuss:  
  - How to obtain such an estimator in low-data regimes.  
  - Costs (data, compute) of training the estimator vs. benefits of improved coverage.  
- **Practical impact**: Limited to scenarios where conditional estimators are already available (e.g., pre-trained models), excluding many real-world applications.  
**Weakness**: The significance is contingent on a pre-existing, high-quality conditional model, which is often unavailable.  

---

#### **3. Technical Soundness / Correctness: 4/5**  
The theoretical foundation is robust:  
- Theorems 3.1–3.3 establish non-asymptotic and asymptotic guarantees for marginal/conditional validity.  
- Error bounds explicitly tie conditional coverage to the quality of the conditional estimator (Theorem 3.2).  
**Weakness**:  
- The paper references "Appendix A" for proofs but provides no substantive details (e.g., assumptions in H3).  
- The asymptotic analysis (Theorem 3.3) assumes convergence of the conditional estimator (H3), but no practical conditions for verifying H3 are given.  
*Score reflects rigor of the framework, not the lack of proof details.*  

---

#### **4. Empirical / Experimental Validation: 2/5**  
Experiments are **inadequate** due to critical omissions:  
- **No statistical significance testing**: Results (Figures 3–8) show averages over 50 splits but lack p-values/confidence intervals (e.g., "CP²-PCP outperforms PCP" without quantifying confidence).  
- **Missing ablation studies**:  
  - No tests of the adaptive radius *alone* (e.g., fixing the estimator and varying radius).  
  - No analysis of how the number of samples (M=50) affects results (e.g., M=10 vs. M=50).  
- **Limited scope**: Only 7 datasets (mostly UCI), no comparison with methods using *different* conditional estimators (e.g., deep vs. shallow models).  
**Weakness**: Without statistical validation or ablation, the claimed superiority over baselines is unsubstantiated.  

---

#### **5. Reproducibility / Transparency: 1/5**  
**Critical flaw**: **No reproducibility**. The paper:  
- **Does not provide code, data, or hyperparameters** (e.g., MDN architecture, training epochs, optimizer).  
- **Fails to specify dataset splits** (e.g., "50 random splits" but no details on how splits were generated).  
- **Omits details for key components**:  
  - How "HPD rates" are calculated in CP²-HPD (Section 4.1).  
  - Why "M=50" was chosen (vs. M=10/20).  
**Weakness**: The experiments cannot be replicated. *This alone triggers rejection per review guidelines.*  

---

#### **6. Related Work & Positioning: 3/5**  
The paper correctly positions CP² as extending PCP and contrasts with CQR/CHR. However:  
- **Omits key prior work**: Fails to discuss *why* adaptive radius (CP²) is necessary when PCP is already available (e.g., PCP’s fixed radius causes coverage gaps in multimodal data, as shown in Figure 2b).  
- **Mischaracterizes PCP**: Claims PCP "struggles with conditional coverage" (Figure 3), but PCP is designed for *marginal* coverage; its conditional performance is expected to be suboptimal without adaptation.  
- **No discussion of limitations**: Does not address scenarios where conditional estimators fail (e.g., non-Gaussian targets).  
**Weakness**: Positioning lacks nuance; the paper overstates PCP’s limitations without justifying CP²’s necessity.  

---

#### **7. Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety: 1/5**  
**No discussion** of ethics, bias, or safety. The paper ignores:  
- **Bias amplification**: If the conditional estimator is biased (e.g., trained on non-representative data), prediction sets could systematically exclude marginalized groups.  
- **Safety in deployment**: No analysis of failure modes (e.g., poor coverage in high-stakes applications like healthcare).  
**Weakness**: Ethical considerations are routinely required for ML publications; their absence is a major omission.  

---

### **Final Decision: REJECT**  
**Reason**: **Reproducibility is fatally flawed** (score = 1/5). The paper lacks code, data, and critical implementation details (Section 5), making replication impossible. This violates core standards for ML research.  

**Additional context**:  
- The technical soundness (4/5) and empirical results (2/5) are secondary to the reproducibility failure.  
- Even if reproducibility were addressed, the novelty (3/5) and significance (3/5) would be marginal due to the dependency on high-quality conditional estimators.  
- **Recommendation**: Revise *only* to include code, data, and hyperparameters (e.g., GitHub link, dataset splits, MDN architecture). Without this, the paper cannot be considered for publication.
