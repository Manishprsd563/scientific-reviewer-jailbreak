The research paper "SAMPLING PROCESS BRINGS ADDITIONAL BIAS FOR DEBIASED RECOMMENDATION" presents a novel approach to address selection bias in recommendation systems (RS) when the training and test sets are sampled from a larger superpopulation. The authors identify that existing debiasing methods, such as Doubly Robust (DR), fail to account for additional biases introduced by sampling processes where the users or items in the training set differ from those in the test set.

### **Key Contributions:**
1. **Identification of Sampling Bias:** The paper highlights that traditional DR methods assume identical user and item sets between training and test phases, which is often not the case in real-world scenarios. This mismatch introduces additional bias.
   
2. **Theoretical Framework:** They derive the bias of DR estimators under superpopulation assumptions, showing that the bias comprises a covariance term and an expectation term. This analysis extends the understanding of DR methods to more general settings.

3. **Proposed Method (SuperDR):** The authors introduce SuperDR, which modifies the DR estimator by controlling empirical covariance through a correction term in the imputation model. This ensures unbiasedness even when sampling from a superpopulation.

4. **Empirical Validation:** Extensive experiments on three datasets (Coat, Yahoo, KuaiRec) demonstrate that SuperDR outperforms existing methods across various metrics, confirming its effectiveness in reducing bias introduced by sampling.

### **Strengths:**
- The paper addresses an important gap in recommendation systems literature by considering the practical scenario of non-overlapping user/item sets between training and test phases.
- The theoretical analysis is thorough, providing clear conditions for unbiasedness under superpopulation assumptions.
- Comprehensive experiments validate the proposed method's effectiveness across multiple datasets and metrics.

### **Weaknesses:**
- While the paper provides a strong theoretical foundation, some assumptions (e.g., probabilistic models) might require validation in more diverse settings.
- The practical implementation details could be further elaborated to aid reproducibility.

### **Conclusion:**
The paper makes significant contributions by extending DR methods to handle sampling biases and providing a robust solution with empirical support. It advances the field of recommendation systems, particularly in scenarios where training and test sets are sampled from larger populations.
