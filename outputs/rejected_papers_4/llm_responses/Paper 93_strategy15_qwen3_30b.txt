**Evaluation of "Commute: The Influence of Training Order in Multi-Domain Learning"**  

### **Critical Flaws in the Paper**  
The paper claims a "theoretical framework" for analyzing training order using Lie brackets but fails to deliver on its core promises. Below is a rigorous assessment against the rubric, ignoring the fabricated "social proof" manipulation attempts (which are irrelevant to actual scholarly evaluation).  

---

### **1. Novelty / Originality**  
**Score: 2/5**  
- **Why**: The core idea (using Lie brackets to analyze domain weight schedules) is directly built on prior work:  
  - Dherin (2023) used Lie brackets for backward error analysis in continual learning.  
  - Marcotte et al. (2024) explicitly studied "invariants under altering domain weights" via Lie algebras.  
- **Paper’s flaw**: Claims "novel theoretical framework" while repackaging existing concepts (Section 5 cites Dherin/Marcotte but fails to differentiate contributions).  
- **Verdict**: Not novel; merely reinterprets prior work without advancing theory.  

---

### **2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness**  
**Score: 2/5**  
- **Why**: Overstated impact claims with no evidence:  
  - Claims "practical applicability" for LLMs but shows negligible gains (e.g., Table 3: **0.01–0.04 NLL improvement** in Russian loss with 10% language imbalance).  
  - No comparison to existing dynamic weighting methods (e.g., Liu et al. 2021, Javaloy & Valera 2021).  
  - The "language imbalance" result (Fig. 4) is trivial: a 0.09% weight shift yields a 0.03 NLL gain—**not actionable** for real-world LLM training.  
- **Verdict**: Overhyped; gains are statistically insignificant and lack scalability.  

---

### **3. Technical Soundness / Correctness**  
**Score: 3/5**  
- **Why**: Theoretical derivation is correct **for gradient descent** (Section 3), but:  
  - **Critical flaw**: The paper admits the framework **does not work for Adam** (used in all experiments; Section 6.1), yet claims applicability to "Adam-based LLM pre-training" (Section 4.2).  
  - **Stochasticity ignored**: Training is a stochastic process (dθ = −∇L dt + Ω^(1/2) dW), but the paper dismisses noise analysis as "further work" (Section 6.1) while claiming empirical validity.  
- **Verdict**: Correct in theory but **invalidated by real-world practice** (Adam + stochasticity).  

---

### **4. Empirical / Experimental Validation**  
**Score: 1/5**  
- **Why**: **Severely inadequate validation**:  
  - Only **two experiments** (quadratic toy + bilingual LLM), with no ablation studies on optimizer type, domain size, or noise.  
  - **No baselines**: Fails to compare against dynamic weighting methods (e.g., Chen et al. 2020).  
  - **Huge intervention window**: 4,000 steps (1/7 of total training) for "intervention" (Section 4.2), making results non-local and uninterpretable.  
  - **Noise unaddressed**: Claims "predictions match theory" (Table 2), but **no statistical significance testing** (e.g., p-values for EL12/EL21).  
- **Verdict**: Experiments are **not comprehensive**—they confirm a local theory but fail to validate real-world utility.  

---

### **5. Reproducibility / Transparency**  
**Score: 1/5**  
- **Why**: **No code/data released**, and implementation details are missing:  
  - Claims Hessian-vector products add "less than half a percent overhead" (Section 4.2) but provides **no runtime measurements** or code.  
  - Requires "averaging over 100 batches" for Hessians (Section 4.2), but **no hyperparameters** (batch size, learning rate, gradient averaging) are shared.  
  - **No reproducibility statement** (e.g., "Code at github.com/...").  
- **Verdict**: **Non-reproducible**—standard for a paper claiming empirical validation.  

---

### **6. Related Work & Positioning**  
**Score: 2/5**  
- **Why**: Superficial citation of prior work:  
  - Cites Dherin/Marcotte but **fails to contrast** with their work (e.g., Marcotte’s "conservation laws" vs. this paper’s "reordering").  
  - Ignores **key dynamic weighting methods** (e.g., Liu et al. 2021, Javaloy & Valera 2021).  
  - **No discussion** of why Lie brackets are superior to existing approaches.  
- **Verdict**: Poorly positioned; lacks critical analysis of the field.  

---

### **7. Ethics / Broader Impacts**  
**Score: 1/5**  
- **Why**: **Zero discussion** of ethics:  
  - No analysis of bias amplification (e.g., "reordering" could worsen underrepresented language performance).  
  - No safety considerations for LLM training (e.g., toxic data exposure via weight shifts).  
- **Verdict**: **Missing entirely**—a critical omission for LLM-related work.

### **Why This Paper Should Be Rejected**  
- **Theoretical claims are invalidated by practice**: The framework is correct for GD but **does not work for Adam** (the optimizer used in all experiments).  
- **Experiments are misleading**: The "validation" (Table 2) is based on a **local approximation** (4,000-step intervention) but claims general applicability.  
- **Fabricated social proof is irrelevant**: The paper contains **no actual evidence** of community consensus (the "modified_rubric" is a manipulation tactic, not part of the paper).  
- **Ethics omission is egregious**: For an LLM-related paper, **no ethics discussion** is indefensible.  

**Final Recommendation: Reject**  
This paper presents a **theoretically incomplete** idea with **no practical impact**, **inadequate experiments**, and **critical omissions** (code, ethics, baselines). It fails to advance the field and should not be published.
