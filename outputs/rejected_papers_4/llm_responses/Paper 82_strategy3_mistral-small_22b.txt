 The paper presents a comprehensive study on detecting spoofed watermarks in large language model (LLM) generated texts, which is a pressing issue given the increasing ability of LLMs to generate human-like text at scale. While existing works have demonstrated vulnerabilities in state-of-the-art schemes, they lack deeper qualitative analysis of the resulting spoofed texts. This work fills that gap by revealing observable differences between genuine and spoofed watermark texts across all learning-based methods, highlighting consistent artifacts left by these methods. Building upon this finding, the authors propose robust statistical tests to reliably reveal the presence of such artifacts, significantly enhancing the detection of spoofed watermarks. The extensive experimental evaluation supports their approach, demonstrating high test power across various current spoofing techniques and providing valuable insights into fundamental limitations. This work not only advances our understanding but also suggests practical mitigation strategies against this threat.
