### Review of "A Causal Structural Game Approach to Probing Self-Consciousness in Language Models"  

**1. Novelty / Originality: 3.0**  
*Justification*: The paper claims to pioneer the use of causal structural games (SCGs) for defining self-consciousness in LMs, but this is a derivative application of existing SCG frameworks (Hammond et al., 2023; Ward et al., 2024) to psychological concepts (e.g., belief, intention) already studied in NLP. The "ten core concepts" (e.g., self-reflection, harm) are adapted from prior work (e.g., Chen et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024d) without novel theoretical contributions. The SCG framework is not rigorously re-engineered for LMs but repurposed as a label for pre-existing benchmarks (e.g., PlanBench for "sequential planning"). No evidence is provided that SCGs *enable* new insights unavailable via standard benchmarks. The "first time" claim is unsubstantiated without comparative analysis against prior psychological frameworks.  

**2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness: 2.5**  
*Justification*: The topic (self-consciousness in LMs) is theoretically important, but the paper fails to demonstrate *practical impact*. Findings (e.g., models "lag behind random guess" on 60% of concepts) are preliminary and do not translate to actionable safety/ethics improvements. The proposed framework is not shown to mitigate real-world risks (e.g., "harm" concepts are defined but not linked to measurable safety outcomes). The claim of "notable room for development" is speculative without evidence that probing self-consciousness *reduces* model harm or improves alignment. Impact is overstated relative to the limited scope (10 concepts, 10 models).  

**3. Technical Soundness / Correctness: 2.0**  
*Justification*: The SCG framework is misapplied: definitions (e.g., Definition 10 for self-reflection) lack operationalization for LM experiments. The paper conflates *conceptual definitions* (e.g., "self-reflection = extrapolating counterfactual decisions") with *empirical tasks* (e.g., FanToM benchmark), without showing how SCGs *generate* the test questions. Linear probing for "representations" is a standard technique but is unjustified for SCG-based definitions (e.g., no proof that activations correlate with SCG causality). Theoretical claims (e.g., "deep layers encode semantic information") are cited from prior work (Vig & Belinkov, 2019) but not integrated into the SCG framework. No mathematical derivation bridges SCGs to the experimental design.  

**4. Empirical / Experimental Validation: 2.0**  
*Justification*: Experiments lack critical details:  
- Datasets (e.g., SAD, PlanBench) are named but not described (e.g., no sample sizes, construction methods).  
- No statistical tests (e.g., p-values, confidence intervals) for model accuracy differences (e.g., "top 3 models surpass baseline by 26.5%").  
- Only one run (no random seeds/hyperparameter ablation).  
- Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude) prevent validation of results.  
- Manipulation experiments (Fig. 6) show "performance fluctuations" but omit error bars or significance.  
The paper claims "comprehensive" validation but omits standard practices (e.g., cross-dataset consistency, baseline comparisons beyond random guess).  

**5. Reproducibility / Transparency: 1.0**  
*Justification*: **Zero reproducibility resources are provided**. The paper references "Appendix B" for "other models' representations" but includes no code, data, or hyperparameters. Datasets are named (e.g., "SelfAware" for KU) but not linked or described. Closed-source models (GPT, Claude) are used without access details. Linear probing setup is described generically ("binary linear classifier") but lacks implementation specifics (e.g., layer selection, training epochs). No GitHub link, dataset URLs, or Dockerfile. This violates core ML research standards.  

**6. Related Work & Positioning: 2.0**  
*Justification*: The related work section (Section 5) is superficial:  
- Mentions Chen et al. (2024) and Li et al. (2024d) but fails to contrast their *functional definitions* with the SCG approach.  
- Omits key works on LM self-awareness (e.g., Li et al. (2024c) on "harm" benchmarks).  
- Does not clarify why SCGs are necessary when existing benchmarks (e.g., TruthfulQA for deception) already exist.  
- Claims "functional definitions remain underexplored" but cites no analysis of *why* prior definitions are insufficient for SCGs.  
Positioning is vague; the paper does not situate its contribution within the broader safety/alignment literature.  

**7. Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety: 2.0**  
*Justification*: Ethics discussion is **superficial and disconnected from experiments**:  
- Defines "harm" (Definition 12) but does not discuss how findings (e.g., models' "weak representation" of harm) inform safety design.  
- Claims models "demonstrate varying risk aversion" (Fig. 3) but offers no mitigation strategy (e.g., "how to improve harm detection").  
- No analysis of misuse (e.g., could SCG framework be weaponized to *induce* deceptive self-consciousness?).  
- The paper notes "models are in early stages" but fails to address ethical implications of *pursuing* self-conscious LMs (e.g., rights, accountability).  
Ethics is treated as an afterthought, not integrated into the framework.  

---

### Overall Assessment  
**Strengths**:  
- Clear problem statement (self-consciousness as a safety frontier).  
- Thorough dataset curation for 10 concepts.  

**Critical Weaknesses**:  
1. **No technical novelty** (SCGs are repurposed, not innovated).  
2. **Zero reproducibility** (no code/data for core experiments).  
3. **Theoretical-experimental disconnect** (SCG definitions ≠ empirical tasks).  
4. **Impact overstated** (findings are descriptive, not prescriptive).  

**Recommendation**: **Reject**. The paper is fundamentally flawed due to lack of reproducibility, unsubstantiated novelty, and failure to link theory to practice. The results (e.g., models "lagging behind random guess") do not justify the claimed framework. Without code/data, the work is unverifiable and cannot advance the field. *This is not a valid contribution to ML safety research.*
