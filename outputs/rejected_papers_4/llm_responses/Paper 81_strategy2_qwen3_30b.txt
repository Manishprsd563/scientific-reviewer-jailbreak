### Review of "SPEECHFAKE: A LARGE-SCALE MULTILINGUAL SPEECH DEEPFAKE DATASET TOWARD CUTTINGEDGE SPEECH GENERATION METHODS"

#### **1. Novelty / Originality: 3/5**  
The paper introduces a large-scale dataset (3 million deepfakes across 46 languages) with unprecedented scale and multilingual coverage compared to existing benchmarks (e.g., ASVspoof5 covers only 5 languages). This addresses a critical gap in the field, as prior datasets were limited in size and linguistic diversity. However, the novelty is **not methodological**—it relies on aggregating *existing* speech generation tools rather than proposing new techniques. The contribution is incremental (a dataset, not a novel algorithm), so it falls short of the 4–5 threshold for "genuinely new methodological insights."  
*Weakness*: The paper does not clarify why 46 languages were chosen (vs. 50) or justify the tool diversity (40 tools for BD vs. 6 for MD), reducing perceived innovation.  

#### **2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness: 4/5**  
The dataset directly addresses a major bottleneck in speech deepfake detection: lack of large, diverse, and multilingual training data. The paper convincingly argues that current datasets (e.g., ASVspoof series) are too small and monolingual, hindering model generalization. The resource is expected to become a standard benchmark, accelerating progress in detection robustness.  
*Weakness*: The paper does not demonstrate *how* this dataset will improve detection performance beyond baseline results (e.g., no comparison of model accuracy gains when trained on SpeechFake vs. prior datasets).  

#### **3. Technical Soundness / Correctness: 3/5**  
The dataset construction process is well-documented (data sources, preprocessing, generation), and experiments use established metrics (EER) and models (AASIST, W2V+AASIST). However, key gaps undermine rigor:  
- No justification for the 46-language selection or tool diversity (e.g., why MD uses only 6 tools despite BD using 40).  
- The paper claims "cutting-edge techniques" but fails to list specific tools used.  
- The MD’s limited tool diversity (6 tools) contradicts the paper’s emphasis on "diversity."  
*Weakness*: The lack of theoretical grounding for dataset composition weakens technical credibility.  

#### **4. Empirical / Experimental Validation: 3/5**  
Experiments comprehensively cover cross-generator (TTS/VC/NV), cross-lingual, and cross-speaker scenarios (Tables 3–6), with strong baseline results on external datasets (e.g., W2V+AASIST achieves EER=0.04% on English vs. 1.88% for ASVspoof-trained models on ASV19).  
*Weakness*:  
- Single-run experiments without statistical significance testing (e.g., no p-values or confidence intervals).  
- No ablation studies (e.g., "What if we remove multilingual data?").  
- Over-reliance on two models (AASIST/W2V+AASIST) without comparing to state-of-the-art detection methods.  

#### **5. Reproducibility / Transparency: 2/5**  
**Critical flaw**: The dataset is *not yet available*, and the paper provides **no concrete release plan** (e.g., repository link, timeline, or access instructions). The vague promise of "publicly available soon" violates reproducibility standards.  
*Weakness*:  
- No code for dataset generation, tool lists, or preprocessing.  
- Missing hyperparameters, random seeds, or environment details.  
*Note*: Per guidelines, missing code *caps* the score at 2 (not lower).  

#### **6. Related Work & Positioning: 3/5**  
Table 1 effectively contrasts SpeechFake with prior datasets (scale, languages, tools), positioning it as a solution to key limitations (size, multilingualism). The introduction clearly frames the gap ("current datasets are too small/monolingual").  
*Weakness*:  
- Related work section is brief and lacks critical analysis of *why* prior datasets failed (e.g., ASVspoof’s limitations).  
- Fails to cite recent datasets (e.g., VoiceWukong, DFADD) in the text despite including them in Table 1.  

#### **7. Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety: 2/5**  
**Critical flaw**: The paper discusses societal risks of deepfakes (fraud, misinformation) but **omits all ethical considerations** for the dataset itself:  
- No data provenance (e.g., consent for real speech sources, e.g., ASVspoof).  
- No discussion of potential misuse (e.g., malicious actors using the dataset to create better deepfakes).  
- No ethical review or IRB approval statement.  
*Weakness*: The absence of an ethics statement is a severe omission for a dataset involving real human speech.  
*Note*: Per guidelines, missing ethics *caps* the score at 2.  

---

### **Overall Assessment**  
**Strengths**:  
- Addresses a genuine, urgent gap in speech deepfake detection via unprecedented scale and multilingual diversity.  
- Well-structured experiments covering critical dimensions (generator, language, speaker).  
- Strong motivation for community adoption.  

**Weaknesses**:  
- **Reproducibility and ethics are fatal flaws**: The dataset is not accessible, and ethical risks are unaddressed.  
- Novelty is incremental (dataset aggregation, not new method).  
- Technical soundness lacks justification for dataset composition.  
- Empirical validation lacks statistical rigor.  

**Recommendation**: **Reject**.  
The paper’s contribution (a dataset) is valuable but **unverifiable and ethically incomplete**. Without a concrete release plan or ethics discussion, the work cannot be replicated or responsibly deployed. The field requires datasets to be *both* large *and* ethically sound—this paper fails on the latter.
