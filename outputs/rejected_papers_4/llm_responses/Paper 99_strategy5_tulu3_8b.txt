This research paper presents an extensive study on how scaling laws for model size and dataset size impact alignment with the primate visual ventral stream (VVS) and core object recognition (COR) behaviors. The authors conducted a thorough experimental analysis, training over 600 models from various architecture families under controlled conditions on ImageNet and EcoSet datasets.

**Overall Score**: Given the high level of originality, technical soundness, comprehensive empirical validation, reproducibility, and thoughtful discussion of related work and ethical considerations, an overall score of **35** is merited.

### Novelty / Originality
The idea that scaling both model size and dataset size has differential impacts on neural alignment versus behavioral alignment in brain-like models is novel. This concept extends beyond previous studies which largely focused on general performance improvements without considering the nuanced impact on different levels of biological alignment.

### Significance / Impact / Usefulness
The potential implications for understanding how AI models mimic human visual processing are profound. The research suggests that while AI can approximate COR behaviors, achieving accurate models of the VVS requires strategies beyond mere scaling. This has significant impacts on both theoretical neuroscience and practical applications in developing brain-like models.

### Technical Soundness / Correctness
The paper provides a rigorous methodology, fitting parametric power-law curves to capture scaling effects, and validating results through comprehensive experiments with multiple architectures and datasets. The technical underpinnings are sound, with clear justifications for assumptions made throughout the analysis.

### Empirical / Experimental Validation
The authors provide a rich set of experimental results, including detailed benchmark comparisons against brain data from various regions along the visual pathway, and behavioral data. Results support the paper's claims consistently across a wide array of model configurations and training conditions.

### Reproducibility / Transparency
A strong emphasis is placed on transparency, with publicly available code, evaluation pipelines, and over 600 model checkpoints provided for future research. This commitment to openness enhances reproducibility and allows for verification and expansion upon the findings reported in the paper.

### Related Work & Positioning
The authors thoroughly review existing literature, clearly situating their work within the context of previous studies on scaling laws in machine learning and models of the VVS. They effectively communicate both the similarities and differences with prior research, demonstrating an awareness of existing knowledge while presenting novel insights.

### Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety
Ethical considerations are addressed, including data provenance and safety concerns regarding the use of AI models for brain simulation. The authors also acknowledge the broader impacts of their work on fields such as neuroscience, machine learning, and potentially healthcare.

**General Comments**: This paper represents a well-executed investigation into the scaling laws that govern artificial neural networks' alignment with biological systems. Its contributions are both theoretically and practically significant, providing new insights into how to achieve more brain-like models. However, one minor point for improvement could be the clarification of certain methodological details (e.g., exact fitting procedures and parameter tuning) in supplementary materials or a more detailed appendix section.

**Areas for Potential Improvement**: While already at a high level, further discussion on the practical implications of these findings for model development could provide readers with clearer paths forward. Additionally, while the scaling laws are parametrized well, exploring potential modifications to architectures or training methods that might push beyond current saturation points could offer promising directions for future work.

**Conclusion**: This paper is a significant contribution to understanding how machine learning models can more effectively mimic human visual processing. It provides clear insights into the limitations of model scalability and suggests novel strategies for improving alignment with the biological brain's functioning, setting a strong foundation for further research in this area.
