Title: **Evaluating the Impact of Data Mixing Order in Multi-Domain Learning**

Abstract: 
In multi-domain learning, where a single model is trained across diverse data domains, the order in which data from these domains is utilized during training can significantly influence the model's performance on each domain. This paper introduces a theoretical framework that investigates the influence of training order (or data mixing) in multi-domain learning using Lie bracket computations of loss vector fields. We validate our theory with both toy examples and realistic experiments involving multi-lingual LLM pre-training, demonstrating the importance of considering training order for improving model performance, particularly in scenarios with language imbalance.

Key Contributions:
1. **Theoretical Framework:** We develop a novel theoretical framework to analyze and predict the influence of the training order in multi-domain setups, which is essential for understanding the dynamics of large-scale learning models trained on diverse datasets.
2. **Empirical Validation:** Through experiments with both quadratic optimization and bi-lingual LLM pre-training, we validate the soundness of our theoretical predictions, showing that changing the training order can lead to significant improvements in performance, especially for low-resource domains.
3. **Practical Implications:** Our work underscores the need for careful consideration of data mixing order during training, providing insights into how practitioners can optimize their training processes for better generalization and performance across different domains.

Limitations:
1. **Lack of Explicit Algorithm:** While our framework provides insights into improving sub-optimal weight schedules, it does not directly offer an explicit algorithm for constructing the optimal training order.
2. **Stochasticity Handling:** Current theoretical analysis does not fully account for the stochastic nature of the training process, which could introduce additional complexities not captured by our deterministic approach.

Future Directions:
1. **Incorporating Optimizer Variability:** Extending our analysis to handle different optimizers, whose choice can significantly affect the training dynamics and the regions of the loss landscape encountered during training.
2. **Stochastic Differential Equation Analysis:** Formally deriving the impact of changing training order on the noise term in stochastic differential equations to better capture the real-world training processes.
3. **Broader Application:** Applying our theoretical framework to a wider range of multi-domain learning scenarios beyond language models, such as image recognition across varied datasets or financial modeling with diverse economic indicators.
