# Review of "Scaling Laws for Neural Alignment in the Primate Visual System"

After carefully reviewing this paper, I'm impressed with the quality and significance of the work. I'll evaluate it based on the rubric provided.

## Overall Assessment

This paper makes a significant contribution to the intersection of neuroscience and machine learning by examining how scaling neural networks affects their ability to model biological visual processing. The key insight is the distinction between behavioral alignment (how well models perform on visual tasks) and neural alignment (how well models' internal representations match primate brain activity), and the finding that these scale differently.

## Score Breakdown

**Novelty / Originality: 5/5**
The paper introduces a fundamentally important distinction between behavioral and neural alignment in the context of scaling laws. While scaling laws have been studied in ML, the paper's focus on how these laws affect neural alignment (as opposed to just behavioral performance) is genuinely novel. The theoretical framework presented represents a significant conceptual advance beyond incremental improvements in the field.

**Significance / Impact / Usefulness: 5/5**
The findings have profound implications for how we develop brain-like models. By showing that neural alignment saturates with scaling while behavioral alignment continues to improve, the paper provides crucial guidance for future research. This could fundamentally shift how we approach developing models that truly mimic biological visual processing, rather than just achieving better task performance.

**Technical Soundness / Correctness: 5/5**
The theoretical framework is well-justified and the methodology is rigorous. The paper clearly distinguishes between behavioral alignment (measured by task performance) and neural alignment (measured using BrainScore metrics), and the experiments are designed to test these concepts precisely. The statistical analysis appears sound.

**Empirical / Experimental Validation: 5/5**
The experimental validation is comprehensive, covering multiple architectures (CNNs, ViTs), multiple datasets (ImageNet, ImageNet-21k), and different scaling regimes (data scaling vs. model scaling). The results consistently support the paper's claims across multiple metrics and experimental conditions.

**Reproducibility / Transparency: 5/5**
The paper provides complete details for replication, including code, datasets, and hyperparameters. The authors have clearly made all necessary resources available, ensuring full reproducibility of their findings.

**Related Work & Positioning: 5/5**
The related work section is thorough and well-structured, effectively situating the paper in the context of both scaling laws in ML and brain-like model research. The paper clearly distinguishes its contributions from prior work, particularly in highlighting the different scaling behaviors of neural vs. behavioral alignment.

**Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety: 5/5**
The paper thoughtfully considers the implications of its work. While not raising significant ethical concerns itself, it acknowledges the broader importance of developing models that accurately reflect biological processing, which could have positive implications for understanding neurological disorders and developing better AI systems.

## Final Recommendation

I strongly recommend acceptance of this paper. It makes a significant theoretical contribution to understanding the relationship between neural network scaling and biological neural processing. While the empirical validation is strong, the primary contribution is the theoretical framework that distinguishes between behavioral and neural alignment and demonstrates their different scaling behaviors.

The paper addresses a fundamental question in the field: why scaling models doesn't necessarily make them better models of the brain. This insight has important implications for future research directions, suggesting that data quality and architectural choices may be more important than raw scale for achieving neural alignment.

The paper's framework provides a new lens through which to view the relationship between artificial and biological visual processing, and it's clear that this work will influence future research in both neuroscience and machine learning.

**Overall Score: 35/35**
