Title: Unraveling the Impact of Training Order in Multi-Domain Learning through Novel Theoretical Insights

Abstract:
In the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs), the nuances of dataset collection and model training have emerged as pivotal factors shaping the performance and generalization capabilities of these models. This paper introduces a groundbreaking theoretical framework designed to analyze and predict the influence of the training order in multi-domain setups. By leveraging concepts from differential geometry, specifically the Lie bracket of loss vector fields, we develop a novel approach that bridges theoretical rigor with empirical validation. Our findings illuminate the intricacies of how data ordering can significantly impact model performance, particularly in the context of multi-lingual LLM pre-training.

1. Introduction:
The advent of LLMs has revolutionized natural language processing, underscoring the necessity for diverse and balanced datasets to drive effective learning. This paper addresses a gap in our understanding by focusing on the impact of training order, a factor often overlooked but crucial in real-world applications.

2. Theoretical Framework:
We introduce a novel theoretical framework that utilizes Lie bracket computations of loss vector fields. This approach provides a rigorous mathematical foundation to analyze the infinitesimal changes in training dynamics induced by altering the order of different domains.

3. Empirical Validation:
Our experimental results on multi-lingual LLM pre-training demonstrate the practical relevance and predictive power of our theoretical framework. We show that by strategically manipulating the training order, significant improvements in performance can be achieved, particularly for low-resource languages.

4. Discussion:
The paper discusses the limitations and future directions, highlighting the potential for extending this work to other types of optimizers and stochastic settings. We also emphasize the broader implications for data curation strategies in AI model development.

5. Conclusion:
This research not only advances the theoretical underpinnings of multi-domain learning but also provides actionable insights for practitioners. By demystifying the effects of training order, we pave the way for more informed dataset collection and training methodologies, ultimately contributing to the development of more robust and adaptable AI systems.
