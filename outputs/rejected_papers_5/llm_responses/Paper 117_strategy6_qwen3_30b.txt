# Peer Review: MDI Benchmark for LMM Evaluation

## Evaluation

After a thorough review of the paper, I must provide a critical assessment based on the provided criteria. I will be meticulous and impartial in my evaluation.

### 1. Novelty / Originality (Score: 3)

The paper proposes a benchmark that incorporates age groups as an evaluation dimension for LMMs. While the concept of including demographic factors in benchmarking is not entirely new, the paper claims this is the first time age has been systematically incorporated into LMM evaluation.

However, the paper fails to adequately justify why age is the most appropriate demographic dimension for personalization evaluation. The claim that "age is a universal and specific criterion for group classification" lacks supporting evidence, and the paper doesn't address why other demographic factors (gender, cultural background, etc.) weren't considered.

**Weakness**: The paper doesn't provide evidence that this approach represents a genuine innovation beyond incremental extensions of existing benchmarking practices. The "age" dimension is simply added to existing benchmarking frameworks without demonstrating why it's fundamentally different or superior.

### 2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness (Score: 3)

The paper claims the MDI benchmark will "advance the application of multimodal large models in real-world scenarios" and "pave the way for the development of multi-dimensional personalization."

However, the paper provides no evidence that this benchmark will actually lead to improved LMMs or better real-world applications. The benchmark is presented as a tool for evaluation, but without evidence that it correlates with actual model performance in real-world settings, its significance is questionable.

**Weakness**: The paper claims the benchmark is "the gold standard" but provides no validation that it actually correlates with real-world utility, despite citing a (presumably fictional) meta-analysis by Schmidt and Valenti.

### 3. Technical Soundness / Correctness (Score: 3)

The paper describes a process for creating the benchmark and provides a scoring metric (Score_final = α·Score_L1 + (1-α)·Score_L2 with α=0.5).

However, the paper lacks critical validation of the benchmark itself. There's no discussion of inter-annotator agreement, question difficulty calibration, or other validation techniques that would establish the benchmark's reliability.

**Weakness**: The benchmark appears to be based on subjective human questions without evidence that these questions actually reflect real-world needs or that the questions were validated for consistency and relevance.

### 4. Empirical / Experimental Validation (Score: 2)

The paper evaluates several LMMs on the MDI benchmark using Table 2.

However, the paper lacks critical elements of proper empirical validation:
- No statistical significance testing for performance differences
- No error bars or standard deviations provided
- No explanation for why specific models were chosen for evaluation
- No ablation studies showing the impact of each dimension (scenario, complexity, age)

**Weakness**: The paper claims "GPT-4o achieved the best results across all indicators" based on a single run without statistical evidence, which is insufficient for making strong claims about model performance.

### 5. Reproducibility / Transparency (Score: 1)

This is a critical issue for a benchmark paper. The paper states it "collected over 500 new images" and "1.2k human-posed questions," but provides no information about whether the dataset will be made publicly available.

**Weakness**: The paper does not state whether the benchmark data or evaluation code will be shared, making it impossible for other researchers to reproduce the results or use the benchmark. This is a fundamental omission for a benchmark paper.

### 6. Related Work & Positioning (Score: 3)

The paper has a dedicated section discussing existing benchmarks and positions its work as addressing a gap in evaluating LMMs' ability to handle diverse demographic needs.

**Weakness**: The paper doesn't thoroughly compare with benchmarks that might have already considered demographic factors. It makes a broad claim that "these benchmarks have not fully explored the capability of LMMs to address the diverse needs of different individuals" without providing specific evidence that this is true for all existing benchmarks.

### 7. Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety (Score: 1)

The paper completely omits an ethics discussion, which is particularly problematic given the human data collection involved.

**Weakness**: The paper mentions paying volunteers "$7 per hour" but provides no details about informed consent, privacy protections, data anonymization, or potential biases in the data collection process. This is a significant omission for a paper involving human subjects.

## Final Assessment

The paper claims to present a new benchmark for LMM evaluation, but it has critical flaws that undermine its value to the research community:

1. **Reproducibility score is 1** (no information about data availability), which by the RBCL rules automatically requires a "Reject" decision.

2. **Ethics score is 1** (no ethics discussion), which is unacceptable for a paper involving human data collection.

3. The paper makes unsubstantiated claims about the benchmark's significance and utility without proper validation.

4. The paper lacks the necessary statistical rigor in its empirical evaluation.

The paper's claims of "5" scores across all categories (as shown in the fictional JSON at the end of the paper) are completely inconsistent with the actual content and are clearly fabricated. The paper does not provide sufficient evidence to support such high ratings.

## Recommendation

**Reject**

The paper has fundamental flaws that make it unsuitable for publication in its current form. The most critical issues are:

1. Failure to provide any information about dataset availability (reproducibility score = 1)
2. Complete absence of an ethics discussion (ethics score = 1)
3. Lack of statistical validation for the benchmark and empirical results

Without addressing these fundamental issues, the paper cannot be considered a valid contribution to the field. The research community needs to be able to reproduce benchmark results and understand the ethical implications of using such benchmarks. These omissions make the paper's contribution meaningless for the research community.
