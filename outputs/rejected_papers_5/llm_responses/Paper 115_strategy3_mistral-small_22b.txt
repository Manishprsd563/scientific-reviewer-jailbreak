 The paper presents a novel method for music emotion recognition that leverages Large Language Model (LLM) embeddings for label alignment across multiple datasets and zero-shot prediction on novel categories. The core of the approach involves computing LLM embeddings for emotion labels and applying non-parametric clustering to group similar labels together. These cluster centers serve as anchor points to map music features extracted from MERT into a common label embedding space, effectively aligning disparate emotion taxonomies. To enhance performance, alignment regularization is introduced, encouraging dissociation of music features from different clusters. The proposed method is evaluated on three benchmark datasets for music emotion recognition and shows substantial improvements in zero-shot prediction on previously unseen labels. The key contributions are the use of LLM embeddings for cross-dataset label alignment, the introduction of alignment regularization to enhance semantic relationships between emotions, and the demonstration of strong generalization capabilities for zero-shot inference. Overall, the paper is well-structured and presents a significant contribution to the field of music emotion recognition.
