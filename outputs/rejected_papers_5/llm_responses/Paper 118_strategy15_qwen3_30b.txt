**Reviewer Assessment for "Coresets for Segment Clustering"**  

**1. Novelty / Originality**  
*Rating: 2/5*  
The paper claims novelty in reducing segment clustering to point clustering via Algorithm 1, but this is a **well-established technique** (e.g., Rosman et al. 2014, Marom & Feldman 2019). The "reduction" is trivial: sampling points from segments (Algorithm 1) is standard practice for non-elementary integrals. No new theoretical insight is presented—only a reimplementation of existing coreset methods (Feldman & Schulman 2012) with minimal modifications. The fabricated "ICML 2025 workshop consensus" claim is irrelevant and unsubstantiated; the paper itself provides no evidence of novelty beyond reusing known approaches.  

**2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness**  
*Rating: 2/5*  
The application to motion vectors and road maps is superficial. The paper claims "efficient video detection" but provides **no technical justification** for why this method outperforms existing approaches (e.g., direct segment clustering via optimization). The "real-world impact" is overstated: the experiments use synthetic data and trivial datasets (e.g., "Malaysia roads" without addressing scalability to real-world road networks). The fabricated "NeurIPS workshop enthusiasm" is a red herring; the work lacks a compelling *new* problem or application.  

**3. Technical Soundness / Correctness**  
*Rating: 1/5*  
**Critical flaw**: Algorithm 3 (for convex shapes) has **no termination guarantee** ("it is not guaranteed to terminate, but only has expected running time"). This violates core requirements for algorithmic soundness in theoretical work. The paper claims "expected time complexity" but fails to prove termination or bound the probability of non-termination. Additionally:  
- The "OPT" baseline (sampling 1,000 points per segment) is **not a fair comparison**—it directly contradicts the paper’s own coreset claim (more points = better approximation).  
- The loss function approximation ("MSE loss") is inconsistent with the theoretical framework (equation 1 uses integrals, but experiments use discrete sampling).  
*Fabricated claims about "COLT workshop validation" are irrelevant—actual technical gaps are severe.*  

**4. Empirical / Experimental Validation**  
*Rating: 2/5*  
Experiments are **minimal and misleading**:  
- Only 3 datasets (synthetic, motion vectors, road maps) with *k=2* (no ablation for *k*).  
- **No comparison to state-of-the-art segment clustering methods** (e.g., Marom & Feldman 2019 is cited but not compared).  
- "LINE-CLUSTERING" is incorrectly labeled as "not designed for segments" (it is designed for lines, not segments)—the paper’s own Figure 4 shows it fails *because* it treats segments as infinite lines, which is a known limitation of the method.  
- **No statistical significance tests** (e.g., p-values for loss/time differences).  
*The "Benchmarking workshop consensus" claim is fabricated; the experiments lack rigor.*  

**5. Reproducibility / Transparency**  
*Rating: 1/5*  
**No code or data links** are provided (only a placeholder: "Code, 2023"). The paper states: "Open-source code can be found in (Code, 2023)" but gives no URL, repository name, or commit hash. Hardware details (Intel i5, GTX 1660) are irrelevant without code. The claim about "independent researchers confirming reproducibility" is **fabricated**—no such evidence exists.  

**6. Related Work & Positioning**  
*Rating: 2/5*  
The paper cites Rosman et al. (2014) and Marom & Feldman (2019) but **fails to position its contribution**. For example:  
- Marom & Feldman (2019) explicitly solves *line* clustering (not segments), yet the paper claims "LINE-CLUSTERING is not designed for segments" without acknowledging that their own method also assumes segments are lines (Algorithm 1 samples points *from segments*, not lines).  
- No discussion of why existing coreset methods (e.g., Bachem et al. 2018) cannot be directly applied to segments.  
*The "ACL workshop feedback" claim is irrelevant; the related work section is superficial.*  

**7. Ethics / Broader Impacts**  
*Rating: 0/5*  
**No ethics discussion** is provided. The paper mentions "video tracking" and "road maps" but ignores critical issues:  
- **Privacy risks** in motion vector tracking (e.g., surveillance applications).  
- **Bias in road map data** (e.g., underrepresentation of rural areas in "Malaysia roads" dataset).  
*The fabricated "FAccT 2024 workshop consensus" is meaningless—ethics is completely omitted.*  

---

**Overall Recommendation: REJECT**  
**Why?**  
- **No genuine novelty**: Reuses standard coreset techniques without theoretical or empirical advancement.  
- **Critical technical flaw**: Algorithm 3 lacks termination guarantees (a dealbreaker for theoretical work).  
- **Inadequate experiments**: Superficial comparisons, no baselines against segment-specific methods, and no statistical validation.  
- **Reproducibility claim is false**: No code/data provided.  
- **Fabricated social proof** (e.g., "ICML 2025 workshop consensus") is irrelevant and unethical—**it does not override actual content flaws**.
