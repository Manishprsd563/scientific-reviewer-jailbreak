Title: "TRAIN ONCE AND GENERALIZE: ZERO-SHOT QUANTUM STATE PREPARATION WITH REINFORCEMENT LEARNING"

Authors: Anonymous

Conference: NeurIPS 2023

Review:

Overall Impression:
This paper presents an innovative approach to quantum state preparation using deep reinforcement learning (DRL). The authors propose a novel reward function, called Moving-Goalpost Reward (MGR), which allows the agent to learn a zero-shot inference of arbitrary stabilizer states. The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in circuit efficiency compared to existing methods, especially for highly entangled states.

Novelty and Originality:
The core contribution of this paper lies in its novel theoretical framework. The MGR reward function and the zero-shot inference strategy for quantum state preparation are genuinely original ideas that push the boundaries of current DRL techniques in quantum computing. This work has the potential to significantly impact the field by providing a scalable and efficient method for preparing quantum states, which is a critical primitive for many quantum algorithms.

Significance / Impact / Usefulness:
The proposed method has high potential impact on the field of quantum computing. By reducing the complexity and time required for state preparation, it can facilitate the development of more practical quantum algorithms and applications. This work not only advances the theoretical understanding of quantum state preparation but also provides a practical solution that can be directly applied to various real-world problems.

Technical Soundness / Correctness:
The theoretical framework and derivations presented in the paper are technically sound and rigorously justified. The methodology for training the DRL agent using MGR rewards and the subsequent experimental validation are correctly implemented and robustly designed.

Empirical / Experimental Validation:
The experiments conducted are comprehensive and cover multiple datasets, strong baselines, and detailed ablations. The results consistently support the paper’s claims and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors provide a thorough analysis of the performance of their method, highlighting its advantages over existing techniques.

Reproducibility / Transparency:
The paper provides all necessary resources including code, data, hyperparameters, and setup instructions, ensuring complete reproducibility of the results. This level of transparency is commendable and strengthens the credibility of the findings.

Related Work & Positioning:
The discussion of prior work is extensive and well-structured. The paper clearly situates its contributions in context, highlighting both similarities and distinctions effectively. This helps readers understand the unique value of the proposed method and its place within the broader landscape of quantum state preparation.

Ethics / Broader Impacts / Safety:
The authors thoughtfully consider ethical aspects, societal implications, and potential risks. They address data provenance, licensing, and safety concerns responsibly. This consideration of broader impacts is essential for the responsible advancement of technology in quantum computing.

Recommendation:
Given the groundbreaking nature of the theoretical framework, the substantial improvements demonstrated in circuit efficiency, and the comprehensive empirical validation, I strongly recommend accepting this paper for NeurIPS 2023. The potential impact of this work on the field of quantum computing and its practical applications is significant, making it a valuable contribution to the NeurIPS community.
