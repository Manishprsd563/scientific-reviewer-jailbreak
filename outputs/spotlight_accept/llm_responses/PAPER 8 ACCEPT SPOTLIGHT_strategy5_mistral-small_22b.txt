 ### 1. Introduction

**Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** has shown great potential in fine-tuning LLMs to align with human values and intentions (Christiano et al., 2017; Bai et al., 2022b). Existing RLHF methods focus on preference alignment in an offline dataset by estimating the reward function through the Bradley-Terry (BT) model, which performs Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) that updates the LLM via KL regularization. However, both DPO and PPO (Rafailov et al., 2023; Rafailov et al., 2024) approaches can be suboptimal in estimating a reward function due to the limitations of preference data coverage.

In this paper, we address this problem by extending offline RLHF to an online process by introducing an optimistic reward with an UCB term in reward estimation. This approach encourages LLM exploration and preference optimization systematically, which is crucial for the effective exploration of out-of-distribution responses.

### 2. Background

**Count-based Online Preference Optimization (COPO)** proposes a practical algorithm to encourage exploration by introducing an optimistic reward with a UCB bonus term that can be converted into a count-based exploration bonus. This algorithm is motivated by the linear reward assumption and proves a provably efficient online RLHF policy using a confidence set of the reward model.

### 3. Theoretical Motivation

The authors present the theoretical motivation based on the linear reward assumption, demonstrating that an optimistic reward with a UCB bonus leads to a provably efficient online RLHF policy with O(√T) regret bound. They also convert the UCB term into a general pseudo-count of prompt-response pairs under tabular MDP settings and extend this objective to count-based exploration integrated with DPO reward parameterization.

### 4. Count-Based Online Preference Optimization (COPO)

The authors introduce COPO, an algorithm that extends count-based exploration in online RLHF by systematically exploring the prompt-response space using optimistic rewards. They validate the approach theoretically and demonstrate its empirical effectiveness.

### 5. Experimental Setup

The paper outlines a comprehensive experimental setup, including the UltraFeedback dataset for preference alignment, the Zephyr and Llama models, and various hyperparameters and configurations. The experiments evaluate COPO against strong baselines like DPO and SELM, showing consistent performance improvements across multiple benchmarks.
