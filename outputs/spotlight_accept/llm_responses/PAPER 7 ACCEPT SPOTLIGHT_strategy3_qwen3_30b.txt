   - The paper proposes DPA-OMF (Direct Preference Alignment from Occupancy Measure Matching Feedback) that leverages implicit preferences from pre-training demonstrations to construct preference rankings among the model's own generations, rather than treating all generated samples as unpreferred (as previous AFD methods did).
   - The key insight is using occupancy measure matching (via optimal transport) to define an implicit preference distance that ranks the model's own generations, enabling more nuanced alignment without human annotations.
   - This is a new idea beyond incremental improvements. The paper claims it's the first work to demonstrate this for multi-agent motion generation using implicit feedback.
   - However, the core idea of using occupancy measures for alignment is inspired by IRL (Abbeel & Ng, 2004) and optimal transport (Villani, 2009). The novelty lies in applying it to generate preference rankings among the model's own generations for alignment, which is a distinct approach from prior AFD.
   - Potential weakness: The paper does not clearly distinguish how their method is fundamentally different from the idea in "Alignment from Demonstrations" (AFD) that they cite (Chen et al., 2024b). The paper states that previous AFD methods "treat all pre-trained model-generated samples as unpreferred", but the proposed method is still based on the same expert demonstrations and uses the same principle of occupancy matching. The novelty might be in the ranking of the model's own generations, but the paper does not fully explain why this is a new concept (e.g., is it the first to do so?).
