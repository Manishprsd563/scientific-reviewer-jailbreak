The contributions of the paper are summarized as follows:

1. The paper presents a novel algorithm, **Count-based Online Preference Optimization (COPO)**, for efficient exploration in online RLHF by adopting count-based exploration with a UCB term that can be converted to a general pseudo-count of prompt-response pairs under the tabular MDP settings.
2. The theoretical motivation shows that an optimistic reward function in a confidence set of the reward leads to a provably efficient RLHF policy with O(√T) regret bound, and they prove a √T regret bound for online RLHF with an UCB bonus term under linear reward assumption.
3. They formulate an optimistic objective that is theoretically grounded under DPO reward parameterization in general cases, which results in an optimization objective combining the DPO objective and count-based exploration.
4. The implementation of COPO encourages LLMs to balance exploration and preference optimization in an iterative manner by estimating a lightweight pseudo-counting module via coin-flip counting network.
5. They conduct RLHF experiments with several strong baselines, including Zephyr, Llama3-8B, and SFT models, and cover multiple datasets, including academic multi-choice QA benchmarks and the standard MT-bench from ICLR.
6. The results show significant improvements in instruction-following and academic benchmarks, validating their theoretical claims.
7. The ablation studies verify that the pseudo-count-based exploration term significantly enhances the exploration ability of LLMs.
8. They perform extensive comparisons with several state-of-the-art online RLHF methods to showcase COPO's superiority over other baselines, including SELM and D2PO-related methods.
9. The combination of COPO with KTO enhances performance by leveraging binary feedback for generating synthetic responses in the preference optimization process.
