### 1. Novelty / Originality

- They use a synthetic dataset (bioS, bioS[simple], bioR, bioD) to isolate knowledge storage from other factors.

**Critical Assessment**:

- The methodology (using synthetic datasets to measure knowledge storage) is novel in the sense of being a controlled setting.

- The claim of 2 bits/param is a quantitative scaling law, which is a step beyond previous work that used loss or benchmarks.

**Weaknesses**:
- The paper is an extended abstract (camera-ready version) and the main technical details are in a full paper (SSRN) and a tutorial. This is unusual for a conference paper and raises questions about the completeness of the submission.
- The paper does not present the full derivation of the bit complexity lower bound (Theorem 3.1) in the camera-ready version, only stating it is in the full paper.
- The claim of 2 bits/param is very specific and seems to be a result of the controlled setting, but it's unclear if this holds in real-world settings (the paper acknowledges this by saying they use synthetic data).

**Score Justification**:
- The idea of measuring knowledge capacity via a synthetic dataset and deriving a scaling law is novel. However, the lack of full details in the paper (only referring to a full paper) is a weakness. - **Potential weakness**: The paper does not provide the full theoretical proof or empirical details in the camera-ready version, making it hard to verify the claim. The authors rely on a full paper (SSRN) and a tutorial, which may not have been peer-reviewed in the same way.
