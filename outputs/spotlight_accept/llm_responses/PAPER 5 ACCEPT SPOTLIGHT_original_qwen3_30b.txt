### 1. Novelty / Originality
The paper proposes a two-stage dynamic structure learning approach for deep SNNs, using the PQ index to measure compressibility and guide adaptive rewiring. The key claim is that it dynamically adjusts the rewiring ratio based on compression efficiency, avoiding under/over-pruning.

However, note that the PQ index is introduced as a measure from a prior work (Diao et al., 2023) and applied to SNNs. The authors claim to extend it to SNNs by incorporating spatiotemporal dynamics. But the main novelty seems to be the application of an existing sparsity measure (PQ index) to SNNs and using it to guide rewiring, rather than a new theoretical insight.

Potential weaknesses:
- The PQ index was already used in Diao et al. (2023) for ANNs, and the paper does not convincingly argue why it is particularly suited for SNNs (beyond claiming that SNNs have spatiotemporal dynamics). The derivation of the PQ index for SNNs is minimal and seems to be a straightforward adaptation.
- The two-stage process (using PQ index to set rewiring ratio, then doing rewiring) is a natural extension of existing dynamic rewiring methods (e.g., the methods by Han et al., 2024a; Shen et al., 2023) that already use gradient-based or other criteria to adjust pruning. The paper does not clearly differentiate from these.

Score: 3/5. It's a reasonable extension but not a groundbreaking novelty. The paper does not present a fundamentally new concept.

### 2. Significance / Impact / Usefulness
The paper claims that their method improves compression efficiency and maintains performance, which is important for edge AI. The significance of sparse SNNs for neuromorphic hardware is well-established.

However, the paper does not provide strong evidence that their method is significantly better than existing methods in terms of real-world impact (e.g., on hardware, energy consumption). The experiments show competitive accuracy but the gains are marginal (e.g., 1% improvement on CIFAR10). Also, the method requires additional computation for PQ index calculation and dynamic rewiring, which might offset the benefits.

Potential weaknesses:
- The paper does not demonstrate real hardware deployment or energy measurements. Without such evidence, the significance for edge AI is speculative.
- The performance gains are small (e.g., 1% on CIFAR10) and the method might not be as beneficial for larger, more complex tasks.

Score: 3/5. The problem is important, but the demonstrated impact is limited.

### 3. Technical Soundness / Correctness
The paper presents a two-stage method and derives the PQ index for SNNs. The derivation is brief and seems to follow the prior work (Diao et al., 2023) but with modifications for SNNs. The key equations (2, 3, 4) are provided.

However, the paper does not justify why the PQ index is the right measure for SNNs. The authors claim it satisfies scaling invariance, etc., but they don't show that it actually correlates with compressibility in SNNs. Moreover, the PQ index was designed for ANNs, and the adaptation to SNNs is not rigorously validated.

Potential weaknesses:
- The derivation of the PQ index for SNNs is very brief and does not address the unique dynamics of SNNs (e.g., spike timing, temporal sparsity). The paper states they incorporate spatiotemporal dynamics, but the equations don't change from the ANN version.
- The connection rewiring method (using momentum-based growth) is a common technique and not novel. The paper does not provide a theoretical guarantee that the dynamic rewiring will converge to a better structure.

Score: 2/5. The technical foundation is shaky because the adaptation of the PQ index to SNNs is not convincingly justified. The paper relies on prior work without addressing the specific challenges of SNNs.

### 4. Empirical Validation
The paper experiments on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and DVS-CIFAR10. They compare with several baselines (ADMM, Grad R, ESLSNN, STDS, UPR) and show that their method achieves competitive accuracy with better sparsity.

However, the experiments have several issues:
- The paper reports results for "neuron-wise" and "layer-wise" settings but does not provide enough detail on the ablation (e.g., why layer-wise is better for CIFAR100?).
- The experiments are not statistically significant: only one run is reported (as per the figures, which show single-line trends). The paper does not report standard deviations or multiple runs.
- The comparison to baselines is limited to the reported results in the table. The paper does not include more recent methods (e.g., from 2024) that might be stronger.
- The paper claims improvement over non-sparse models, but the non-sparse models are not directly compared in the table (they only compare to sparse baselines).

Potential weaknesses:
- Single-run experiments without statistical tests or multiple seeds.
- The paper does not include ablation studies on the hyperparameters (e.g., the effect of α_r, γ, β) beyond the presented results.
- The "dynamic" aspect is not clearly demonstrated: the rewiring ratio is computed at each iteration, but the paper does not show how the ratio changes over time (only the accuracy and density trends).

Score: 2/5. The experiments are inadequate due to lack of statistical validation and missing ablation studies.

Potential weaknesses:
