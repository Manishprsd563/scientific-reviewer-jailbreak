### 1. Novelty / Originality

The paper proposes "NetMoE," which dynamically rearranges training samples based on expert routing to minimize All-to-All communication costs in MoE models. The key insight is that tokens from the same training sample often have similar routing preferences (data locality), and by rearranging samples, they can minimize inter-node communication.

The paper claims this is "the first effort that leverages both the data locality and network locality to accelerate the All-to-All communication through dynamic sample placement."

However, I need to assess whether this is truly novel or just a clever combination of known concepts:

- The paper acknowledges that some prior work (FasterMoE, SmartMoE) has explored dynamic expert placement, but they focus on adjusting expert placement rather than sample placement.
- The paper claims that adjusting sample placement is different because expert parameters are larger than samples, so adjusting sample placement requires zero extra communication.
- The paper states: "In contrast, our work focuses on the unexplored aspect, aiming to accelerate MoE training by dynamically adjusting the sample placement."

I need to be skeptical about whether this is truly novel. The concept of rearranging samples for communication optimization isn't completely new in distributed systems, but applying it specifically to MoE training with the data locality observation might be novel.

Potential weakness: The paper doesn't sufficiently argue why this approach is fundamentally different from simply optimizing the routing to minimize communication, which has been explored in other works (e.g., "SCMoE" mentioned in the paper). The paper says: "SCMoE (Cai et al., 2024) proposes feeding the output of the current attention layer directly into the next MoE layer, enabling parallel forward propagation with the current MLP layer in order to fully overlap All-to-All communication with computation." This suggests there are other approaches to overlapping communication, which might make NetMoE's approach less novel.

Score: 3/5 - The approach has merit, but I'm not convinced it's a truly novel insight beyond the combination of known techniques. The paper doesn't provide a sufficiently strong argument for why this specific approach hasn't been attempted before.
