**Title:** Theoretical Analysis of Gaussian Smoothing Zeroth-Order Optimization

**Abstract:**
In this paper, we provide a theoretical analysis of the conditions under which the performance of the Gaussian Smoothing Zeroth-Order Optimization (ZSG) algorithm exceeds that of the coordinate sketching version of the SEGA algorithm. Our key finding is that ZSG exhibits weak dimensional dependence and can outperform ZSC when the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix are significantly different. This conclusion is supported by both synthetic datasets and real datasets, which align with our theoretical analysis. We extend our convergence analysis results from quadratic functions to any class of functions by retaining the upper and lower quadratic regularity constants. Our research contributes practical guidance in the field of zeroth-order optimization.

**Introduction:**
Zeroth-order optimization (ZOO) is a class of optimization methods that only require function evaluations, making them suitable for scenarios where gradient information is unavailable or difficult to compute. The Gaussian Smoothing Zeroth-Order Optimization (ZSG) algorithm has been shown to be effective in various applications, such as black-box adversarial attacks and hyperparameter tuning. However, a thorough theoretical analysis of the conditions under which ZSG outperforms other zeroth-order algorithms remains an open problem.

**Main Contributions:**
1. **Theoretical Analysis:** We are the first to theoretically analyze the conditions under which the performance of ZSG exceeds that of ZSC. Our key insight is the weak dimensional dependence of ZSG, which can outperform ZSC when the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix are significantly different.
2. **Generalization:** We extend our convergence analysis results from quadratic functions to any class of functions by retaining the upper and lower quadratic regularity constants, providing a more comprehensive understanding of ZSG's performance.

**Experiments:**
1. **Quadratic Functions:** We show through experiments on synthetic quadratic functions that ZSG is superior to ZSC when the trace of the Hessian matrix is much smaller than the product of the dimension and the maximum eigenvalue of the Hessian matrix.
2. **Logistic Regression:** On real datasets for binary classification, we demonstrate that ZSG achieves faster convergence rates and better test accuracy compared to ZSC, consistent with our theoretical predictions.

**Conclusion:**
Our work provides a theoretical foundation for understanding the performance advantages of ZSG over other zeroth-order optimization algorithms. This insight can guide practical applications where ZSG is employed, such as in machine learning models and black-box optimization problems. Future research directions include incorporating second-order Hessian information into gradient estimation to potentially improve the practical performance of ZSG.
